# Milestone — As playable as Minecraft Beta

**Status:** milestone, binding. Owner direction, 2026-08-26:
*"new milestone goal: become as playable as the beta versions of minecraft. only, translate the
impact of the respective time each game is in."*

## What is being asked

Minecraft Beta was crude for 2010 and revelatory anyway. Its impact did not come from fidelity — it
came from being **complete and self-directed**: two mouse buttons did everything, you could see what
you were carrying, and a person with no documentation could pick it up and play for six hours.

Translating that to 2026 does not mean blocks and a crafting grid. It means the same completeness,
at what a person now expects of an interface and a world. The bar is not "has more systems than
Minecraft Beta" — Earth Game already does. The bar is **a stranger can play it**.

## Where we actually are

The simulation is years ahead of the game. Late-winter thermoregulation, friction ignition, a
landscape made by its own erosion, nine plant species contesting sites, food that has to be roasted
and pounded, cord whose strength comes from the staple length of the fibre. 199 tests.

And **twenty keys are bound.** X dig, C eat, T roast, P pound, K cut fibre, L work fibre, Z sleep,
E interact, B place site, G add debris, V clear hearth, Q drop, F fly, R respawn, 1 select, Escape,
Space, Shift, Ctrl, F9. Every system that shipped brought its own verb. Nobody can play this without
reading the source, which is the exact opposite of the thing being asked for.

## The gap, in order of how much it costs

| | Minecraft Beta had | Earth Game has |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Verbs** | two mouse buttons, always | twenty keys, one per system |
| **2. Inventory** | a window you open and look at | a text line and number keys |
| **3. Building** | place anything anywhere | one shelter type at fixed sites |
| **4. Sound** | footsteps, breaking, water, mobs | silence |
| **5. Feedback** | the block cracks as you mine it | a percentage in the corner |
| **6. Somewhere to go** | endless world | a 2.4 km catchment |

## The rule that fixes the verbs

**What an action is should come from what you are looking at and what is in your hand** — never from
which key you memorised. That is the same principle the materials already follow: a stone is not
"a hammer" because something stamped it, it is a hammer because of its toughness and grain.

- **Hold left mouse — work on it.** Stone in hand + rhizome = pounding. Empty hand + ground that
  grows tubers = digging. Hand on a stringybark = stripping bark. Fibre in hand = dressing it, then
  laying it. Drill and hearth = making fire.
- **Right mouse — use it.** Eat what is in hand, drink at the water, pick it up, put it down, lie
  down on the bed.
- **Tab** — what am I carrying.
- **Wheel / 1–9** — what is in my hand.
- **WASD, Space, Shift, Escape** — the things everyone already knows.

Six keys and a mouse, and the game tells you what the button will do before you press it.

## Order of work

1. **The action model.** One list of things the founder can do, each knowing when it applies and
   what it costs. The crosshair says what the button would do. Every existing verb ports onto it.
2. **Hands and inventory.** A window, and a held item that means something.
3. **Sound.** Footsteps by surface, fire, wind, water, digging, breaking. Cheap and enormous.
4. **Building that is not a site.** Place what you gathered where you want it.
5. **Onboarding with no tutorial** — the tablet, diegetically, as the thing that explains.

Nothing in the simulation changes. This is entirely about the game reaching the person playing it.
