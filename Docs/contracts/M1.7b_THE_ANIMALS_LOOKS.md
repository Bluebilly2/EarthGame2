# Contract M1.7b — The animals' looks

**Status:** opened and built 2026-09-16, after M1.7a stood the animals up and M1.7c gave them their flight. It is the
part of `M1.7_ANIMALS.md` that waited on William's decision, and he made it on 2026-09-14 (CANON ruling 29: "i dont want
to outsource the animals looks, we will do them") and set the bar on 2026-09-15: "they dont have to be fancy, just
recognisable as what they are." Owner: Claude (Opus 5). **The frames are not taken here and are owed** (below).
**Amended 2026-09-18:** the rigid pieces of promises 1 and 3 became one skin a kind, on William's word about the first
frames; the section at the end says what changed, what was found and how it is proved. The exit record of 2026-09-16
stands as the record of the pieces.

## What was found

- **Nothing is drawn.** M1.7a mirrors every animal and `EntityViews` passes over it — no cube, nothing for the crosshair
  to meet — and M1.7c runs a startled mob at 7 m/s and a pair at 15 m/s with nobody able to see either.
- **No model can be had.** The research of 2026-09-13: no pied oystercatcher model exists to buy or download, no kangaroo
  on sale names a hopping animation, AI generators make neither a hop nor a peck, and Unity does not instance a skinned
  mesh. Ruling 29 settled it: the looks are made here, as the trees and the litter are.
- **The pattern to follow.** `StandForms` is the one engine-free table of what a tree looks like and `StandMeshes` grows
  it; `ItemLooks` gives a stick and a cobble the stand's own mesh. Colour lives in a mesh's corners because the renderer
  takes no `MaterialPropertyBlock` (ARCHITECTURE §8), and the stand's shader assumes a uniform scale and reads no inverse
  matrix, so a piece drawn at scale one keeps its lighting honest.
- **The panel's animal rows were greyed stubs** (M1.D): two buttons that did nothing, with a note saying they waited for
  the looks.
- **The measurements** (research of 2026-09-13, behind `AnimalFlightRules`): an eastern grey kangaroo stands about 1.3 m
  hunched and reaches 1.6 to 1.8 m upright, with a tail about a metre, and hops at about 7 m/s when it flees with strides
  of several metres and a cycle of roughly 0.45 s. A pied oystercatcher is 45 to 50 cm from bill to tail, black above and
  white below, with a long straight orange-red bill and pink legs, and flies at 15 m/s.

## What this slice promises

1. **Shapes made here, engine-free and testable without Unity.** `AnimalShapes` (ClientCore) describes each kind as a
   short list of rigid pieces — a box, a capsule or an ellipsoid with a local place, size, turn and colour — for each of
   the three poses the wire carries (resting, grazing, fleeing).
   - A piece keeps its shape, size and colour in every pose: a pose moves the joints of a skeleton of fixed bone lengths
     and never resizes anything.
   - The two sides of an animal mirror each other exactly.
   - Sizes sit inside the species' published bands, and the tests hold them there.
2. **The moving pose is a function of time.** The kangaroo's hop raises and lowers the body, swings the legs through and
   lifts the tail as a counterweight; the oystercatcher's flight raises the body a metre or so off the ground, puts the
   wings out and beats them, and trails the legs.
   - The hop's cadence is the stride divided by the pace, so it is the measured 0.45 s at the 7 m/s a mob flees at.
   - Each animal starts its cycle at a place of its own drawn from its id, so a mob does not bound as one machine.
3. **Drawn in Unity from meshes made at runtime.** `AnimalLooks` grows one mesh a piece at its own size with its colour in
   its corners, built on first use and kept, in a copy of the stand's material; `EntityViews` gives an animal an object
   with a child for each piece and places the pieces every frame for the pose the server last gave it.
   - Colours plain: a grey-brown kangaroo with a paler chest and dark muzzle, ears, feet and tail tip; a black-and-white
     oystercatcher with an orange-red bill and pink legs.
   - An animal is never offered to the crosshair, as M1.7a promised.
4. **A developer can set one down and look at it.** The panel's two greyed animal rows become deeds: one kangaroo, or one
   oystercatcher, stood two metres ahead of the founder who asks and broadside to them, server-authoritative exactly as
   the stick and the cobble are.
   - Its id names no kind, which is a part of the reserved range presence can never reach, so it cannot collide with a
     mob's ids; the refresh keeps it where it was put instead of taking it away; and it is transient like every animal,
     so nothing of it is saved or named by the world's digest.
   - A row under **The animals** shows every hand-set animal in a pose (1 resting, 2 grazing, 3 fleeing), because a
     hand-set animal is not presence's and never takes flight of its own, and the hop is the look that most needs judging.
5. **A `looks` scenario that puts them in front of the owner.** In a development game it sets the clock to ten in the
   morning, sets down one kangaroo and one oystercatcher, backs off to about three metres and about eight from each and
   captures `roo-near`, `roo-far`, `bird-near`, `bird-far`, then puts them into the fleeing pose and captures
   `bird-flying` and `roo-fleeing`, with a `looks` record for every frame and an end record.
6. **Tests.** Part counts and names per kind and pose; a piece's shape, size and colour unchanged through every pose;
   every size positive, finite and smaller than any part of either animal; left and right mirrored; the heights, lengths
   and span inside the published bands; the animals standing on the ground they are given; the hop's cadence from the
   flee speed and the stride, and the rise and the leg swing it makes; the wingbeat and the flight's height; a grazing
   animal's head lifting and a resting one still; a kind with no shapes refused and an unknown pose drawn resting.

## Non-goals

A skeleton that bends, skinning or blend shapes. A walk, a peck, a graze that takes bites, a head that turns to watch a
founder, or a take-off arc: a fleeing bird is drawn already up. Sounds (M1.8). An animal that can be touched, hunted or
bumped into. Fur, feather or eye detail; a wing bar; a joey. Far forms or a drawing distance of their own: an animal is
drawn wherever the server shows it. The frame's cost measured against §8's lines, and the owner's eyes on the frames —
both owed below.

## How it is proved

`dotnet test Engine/EarthGame.slnx` and `dotnet build Engine/EarthGame.Unity.Compile.csproj`, with the counts as run;
each new test's reason broken once in a way that still builds and seen to go red, then restored; the `looks` scenario run
by William on a built player, and the frames judged by his eyes (STANDARDS 15). No frame is taken by the agent that wrote
this.

## What changed on the way (2026-09-18): one skin in place of the pieces

**Why.** William looked at the frames of 2026-09-16 and said: "the animals in the screenshots look like a load of 3d
shapes put together, can they be made more smooth and connected?" An agent began the answer that day and its session
ended mid-work, with five of its geometry tests red on the branch `wip/agents-2026-09-16`; the work was finished and
proved on 2026-09-18 in a worktree of its own, for the head developer to review, build, record and land.

**What is different.** Promises 1 and 3 no longer describe rigid pieces. An animal is still a skeleton of fixed bone
lengths whose joints a pose moves — the same joints, the same three poses, the same cadence and start-of-cycle by id —
but the skin over it is one connected surface a kind, grown once in the resting pose:

- a few closed shells (the kangaroo's: the body from the tail's tip to the nose, and a haunch, a shank, a foot, an arm
  and an ear a side; the oystercatcher's: the body from the tail to the skull, the bill, and a wing, a leg and a foot a
  side), each a tube of rings — twelve round a body, six to ten round a limb — run along a line of waypoints on the
  bones, with a radius that changes gently along it, mitred where the line bends (a ring at a bend lies in the plane
  between the two runs and is stretched by the secant of half the turn, so the tube keeps its width round the corner) and
  rounded off at both ends by two cap rings and a pole;
- every limb rooted inside its parent, its rounded root buried in the flank or the skull, and the sharpest joints (the
  kangaroo's knee and heel, the bird's ankle) made as one ball nested in another about the same centre, so a knee folded
  flat reads as one rounded knuckle in every pose;
- a belly line round each ring of a body, below which the paler colour is worn (the kangaroo's chest, the
  oystercatcher's white), and every face shaded a little by its own hash, as the trees' faces are;
- each point of the skin riding the bone its ring was laid on, and a ring at a joint shared between the two bones that
  meet there, so the skin bends with the bones the way the graphics card bends it (linear blend skinning), and
  `AnimalShapes.Skin` writes that arithmetic out for the tests to measure a pose by;
- the corners shared between faces as `StandMeshes` shares them: each face leads with a corner carrying its own plane
  and colour and borrows the other two, so the skin has as many corners as faces.

In Unity, `AnimalLooks.Body` turns the skin into one mesh a kind with its bone weights and bind poses, built on first
use and kept; `AnimalLooks.Dress` gives an animal's object a child transform for each bone and a skinned renderer
drawing that mesh over them in the animals' material, casting a shadow; `EntityViews` places the bones every frame for
the pose the server last gave the animal, and the graphics card moves the skin. One renderer an animal, where the pieces
were nineteen or fourteen.

**Finished on 2026-09-18** — the branch's five red tests, and what was found beside them:

- *The folded wing's frame flipped at the wrist* (`EveryShellIsClosedWithNoOpenEdge`, `EveryFaceIsWoundOutwardInEveryPose`).
  The wing's frame took the bird's fore-and-aft line as the hint for its chord, and a folded wing lies almost along that
  line, so the residual that squares the hint to the bone was tiny and pointed down for the wing bone and up for the
  pinion: the band between them was twisted through a half turn. A folded wing now takes the bird's up for its chord
  (the flat of the wing up the flank) and a spread wing the fore-and-aft line (the flat level in the air), which is the
  quarter turn a real wing makes about its own length as it opens; no bone comes near either line in the pose that uses
  it.
- *A straight run's ring came out a hair narrow* (`TheRadiusProfileHasNoSteps`). Rounding put the cosine of half a
  nought-degree turn a hair over one and its secant a hair under, and the test refused a stretch of 0.99999999999999978.
  The cosine is held to one.
- *The inside test missed the wedge at a bend* (`EveryLimbIsRootedInsideItsParentInEveryPose`; the test's own method
  was wrong, not the skin). It judged "inside" by slabs square to the line between ring middles, which leave a wedge
  uncovered on the outside of every mitred bend, and called the kangaroo's own hip outside its rump. It now casts three
  lines from the point through the parent's own posed faces and counts the crossings — odd for inside, the three voting
  — which is the definition of inside for a closed surface, worked out from the faces alone.
- *The bird's belly was never judged* (`TheColoursAreTheSpeciesOwn`; the test's expectation was wrong). It looked for
  faces six centimetres below the rump, and a wader's body is not that deep; the heights are now taken from the spine
  where the face is, and the two lines sit within the body's own depth.
- *The shoulder moved back.* With the inside test honest, the folded wing's rounded root broke the skin where the body
  narrows to the breast; the shoulder sits at 0.62 of the body rather than 0.72, near its widest ring.
- *Feet on the ground.* The skeleton's heights were set for bone lines: the bird's foot stood 2.4 cm above the ground it
  was given and the kangaroo's heels 3 to 4 cm in it. The hips and the rump are set so the skin's underside meets the
  ground, and `TheAnimalsStandOnTheGroundTheyAreGiven` holds every standing pose within two centimetres of it (it
  allowed five below and six above).
- *Ends where the bones end.* Both tails' rounded ends overshot the tail bone by the cap's length, alone among the
  shells; each tip ring now stands a cap short of the bone's end, as the nose's and the bill's do.
- *No strings a frame.* A pose is built every frame for every animal drawn; the paired bones' names are made once.

**Measured, from the skin's own points** (read by a scratch program off `AnimalShapes.BodyOf` and `AnimalShapes.Skin`,
the calls the tests make; the tests hold the bands). The kangaroo is 1,736 faces and 1,736 corners in eleven shells on
seventeen bones; lying up it stands 0.77 m, grazing 0.95 m, at a hop's touchdown 1.24 m and at the top of the hop
1.56 m; it is 2.06 m from nose to tail and 0.48 m across, and its lowest point is within 7 mm of the ground in every
standing pose. The oystercatcher is 996 faces and 996 corners in eight shells on thirteen bones; standing it is 0.31 m
tall and 0.496 m from the bill's tip to the tail's, 0.15 m across with its wings folded, spans 0.80 m with them level,
and flies between 1.20 and 1.61 m off the ground through a beat. Against the pieces: 2,208 faces and 6,624 corners on
nineteen renderers for the kangaroo, 1,084 and 3,252 on fourteen for the oystercatcher.

**How it is proved.** `dotnet test Engine/EarthGame.slnx -c Debug`: 697 passed, 0 failed, exit 0, no warnings, of which
27 are `AnimalShapesTests`; `dotnet build Engine/EarthGame.Unity.Compile.csproj -c Debug`: 0 warnings, 0 errors. Each
of the 27 tests' reasons was broken once in the generator in a way that still built, by a script that edits one line
(two for two of them), runs that one test, restores the file and checks its hash: 27 of 27 red as expected, in two
runs of fourteen and thirteen cases, the file's hash the same before and after each run. Nothing was built in Unity
and no frame was taken here: the head developer builds the harness copy, runs `Tools/world/looks.py` and takes the
frames to William.

**Owed.** The frames and William's eyes (STANDARDS 15); what an animal costs a frame (DEBTS, amended); and one question
for his eyes rather than a debt: the folded wingtips reach about a hand's length past the tail, because the wing is two
bones folded back from the shoulder where a real wing folds three in a Z.
