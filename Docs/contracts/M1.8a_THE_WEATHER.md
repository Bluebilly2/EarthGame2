# Contract M1.8a — The weather

**Status:** closed 2026-09-13, the exit record at the end; opened the same day on William's "up to you", when asked which bigger piece of the game to start while
M1.7a's corpus ran: the first step towards surviving the first night, because the night's cold comes from the weather.
Nothing is drawn; rain on the screen, the darkening ground and the soundscape are M1.8b's. Owner: Claude (Opus 5).

## What was found (2026-09-13)

- **None of the weather is in EarthGame2.** ARCHITECTURE §3 says `Climate`, `Synoptic` and `Weather` are ported, and
  no engine package holds any of them: the line described the plan as done. This slice's first commit corrects it.
- **The first Earth Game built it and tested it.** v1's `Climate` (the year's and the day's temperature, the wind, the
  sky's temperature, the sun's heat), `Synoptic` (one seeded index read as a function of time: rain in fronts and in the
  showers inside them, the cold snap behind a front, the wind, the cloud and the humidity) and `Weather` (the sky at an
  instant, assembled once and read by everyone), held by `WeatherTests` against a weather station three kilometres from
  its wake.
- **That station stood in the highlands.** Moss Vale AWS, 678 m: an August night near 3.0 °C, 1.5 °C pre-human.
  Bherwerre's nearest long record is Point Perpendicular Lighthouse across the bay (Bureau station 068034, 85 m, closed
  2004): in August 16.8 °C by day and 9.5 °C by night, 83.5 mm of rain on 6.0 days; in the year 20.2 and 13.8 °C,
  1,126.2 mm on 106.9 days (the Bureau's 1991–2004 figures as reproduced on Wikipedia's Jervis Bay Village page). Nowra
  (HMAS Albatross, 109 m, 2000–2024), inland, has 18.4 and 7.0 °C and 74.5 mm on 5.4 days of 1 mm or more in August.
  **A pre-human late-August night at Bherwerre bottoms out near 8 °C, six and a half degrees milder than the first
  game's.**
- **The Bureau still refuses scripts.** Its pages answered this machine with 403, so the figures above are the
  reproduction's until the Bureau's own are read in William's browser (DEBTS, "station normals are hand-pulled").
- **v1's shapes do not fit a coast.** Its seasonal mean is a formula of latitude alone, which put Moss Vale within half
  a degree and puts no particular coast anywhere near; its daily range is Moss Vale's 10.4 °C, where the lighthouse's is
  six to seven; and its one cosine puts the night's coldest hour at 03:00, not at dawn (v1's calibration record, "a
  separate defect").
- **The rain's record straddles the drying.** v1 undid the Bureau's cool-season decline since 1994 for a station window
  lying wholly inside it; eleven of the lighthouse's fourteen years lie inside it, and three before.

## What this slice promises

1. **The climate of the place, pre-human, as its weather reads.** The weather's days, fronts and all, give the
   lighthouse's own twelve months of mean daily maxima and minima, read as the Bureau reads them (the highest in the 24
   hours from 9 am, the lowest in the 24 hours to 9 am), less the region's warming since records began (1.51 °C, *State
   of the Climate 2024*, as v1 cites): August to twice the warming's uncertainty, every month to a degree, and every
   month's daily range to v1's 0.15 °C. The day under the weather is fitted to the lighthouse's extremes less what the
   fronts add to them, its mean as one yearly wave and its range month by month, and the air falls with height above
   the station at the lapse rate.
2. **Nights coldest at dawn.** The day warms on a sine from sunrise to an early-afternoon maximum, and the night cools
   on an exponential to the next sunrise (Parton and Logan 1981, *Agricultural Meteorology* 23: 205–216), over the
   month's own daily range.
3. **Rain in fronts and showers.** v1's seeded index with both of its bands, its cold snap, wind and humidity; its
   threshold, its scale and its season re-solved against the lighthouse, the season changing how hard it rains as well
   as how often: over many world seeds, the modelled August and year within a tenth of the pre-human totals, and the
   station's rain days lying between the model's counts at 1 mm and at 0.2 mm, for August and for the year. The drying
   is undone for the share of the record that lies inside it, and the arithmetic is asserted step by step.
4. **A function of time.** The same seed, place and instant give the same weather, asked in any order, so the tablet
   can later ask about five o'clock tomorrow.
5. **One sky.** `Weather.At` assembles the air with its cold snap, the wind and where it blows from, the cloud, the rain
   and the humidity once, and nothing else works any of them out.
6. **The world's, and nobody's to send.** The weather follows from the world's seed and its clock: the server's world
   carries it, and a client asks the same function with the seed its Welcome already carries. Nothing new goes on the
   wire and nothing is saved.
7. **Checked apart from the code.** A tool writes a year of weather for each of many world seeds at the lighthouse's
   height, every half hour, and `weather_check.py` restates the lighthouse's figures and recomputes from those years,
   with its own arithmetic, August's mean maximum and minimum over the Bureau's windows, its daily range, the hour its
   nights are coldest, and the rain's totals and days for August and the year, both numbers on every row.
8. **Tests.** v1's harness, ported and moved to the lighthouse, over many world seeds: the pre-human level and the daily
   range, read from the weather; the coldest hour at dawn; the rain's totals, days and season, the cool half of the
   year raining more and harder as the lighthouse's does; fronts with dry spells between; showers, not drizzle, by
   their hours and by their breaks; most hours dry; one sky whose readings agree; a cold snap that is an air mass,
   changing over days and not hours; the rain's season kept off the temperature; the weather as a function of the seed
   and the time, with no seam at the new year; and a client's sky, from what its Welcome carried, the server's.

## Amended in the doing (2026-09-13)

- **Each month's own range.** A yearly wave for the day's range, as the first commit had, left August's half a degree
  narrow, because the lighthouse's range widens 1.2 °C from July to August, and a second harmonic still left it 0.4;
  promise 2's "the station's own daily range for the month" is now kept to the letter.
- **The weather answers to the station, not the curve.** `weather_check.py`, reading the modelled years as a station's
  thermometers would, found August's nights 0.6 °C cold and its range too wide: the fronts widen a day's extremes, and
  the curve had been fitted as if it were the station's means. Read as the Bureau reads its extremes, at 9 am, the
  widening is a tenth to four tenths of a degree month by month; the curve is now fitted to the lighthouse's extremes
  less it, and promise 1's level and range are held on the weather.
- **How hard, as well as how often.** Fitted with v1's season, which changes only how often fronts come, the
  lighthouse's months came out misshapen: its cool half rains about four tenths harder on each rain day than its warm
  half, on about as many days. Promise 3's season now changes both.
- **Many seeds.** Eight seeds, v1's harness, let the rain's fit chase their luck, 11 to 27 mm a month; the tests and the
  check read many, and the settings were fitted on others.
- **The world's own time.** v1's index, read at the day of the year, jumped at every new year's midnight and repeated
  each year; the weather reads the world's continuous local time.
- **Showers by their breaks.** At the lighthouse's threshold the frontal band without its showers rains about eleven
  hours on a wet day, inside v1's bound of fourteen, but in one unbroken spell, so the test counts the breaks as well.

## Non-goals

Rain drawn, the ground darkening, cloud in the sky, and sound (M1.8b). Things getting wet and drying (v1's W1b), wet
skin, and the body the weather is cold to (M2). Wind shaped by the ground, the sea breeze, and which way a shelter faces.
Snow. The tablet's page for the weather (M1.9).

## Owed

The Bureau's own tables for 068034 and 068072, read in William's browser, in place of the reproduction: holding
August's 6.0 rain days, one yearly wave for how often fronts come gives July and September two to three fewer than the
lighthouse's table, and a longer record would say which of those months are a short record's noise. A wind record for
this coast; until one is read the wind keeps v1's daily shape and the fronts' strength. A daily record for the cold
snap's depth, which is v1's, set against a highland station, and which also sets how far the fronts widen a day's
extremes (DEBTS).

## How it is proved

`dotnet test` and the Unity-layer compile, the count as run; `python Tools/unity/editmode.py`; `weather_check.py` on
modelled years, green, both numbers on every row.

## Exit record (2026-09-13)

**What exists.** `Climate`, the day under the weather, from the lighthouse's table less what the fronts add to a day's
extremes; `Synoptic`, v1's fronts and showers with the rain re-solved; `Weather.At`, the sky assembled once; the world's
`Climate` and `Synoptic`, built when first asked; `WeatherYears` and the almanac tool's `+weather` (`eg2.weather`,
version 1); `weather_check.py`; and the M1.8a gate. Nothing of the weather is drawn, heard or felt, and no founder meets
it yet: a cold night, a wet tinder bundle and a tested windbreak are later slices'.

**Tested, and how.**

- `dotnet test`: 460 passed. The weather's tests read 256 world seeds for the rain and 128 for the air, the air every half
  hour and its extremes at the Bureau's 9 am; the rain's settings were fitted on other seeds.
- Each new test's reason was broken once, the test went red, and the code was put back: the rain no harder in the cool
  season; the showers deleted; every month given the year's mean range; the fronts' widening not taken out; the rain's
  season let into the air; the index read at the day of the year, as v1 read it; the world's fronts drawn from another
  seed; the years sampled at each step's start.
- The Unity-layer compile: 0 warnings, 0 errors. Edit-mode: 8 of 8.
- `weather_check.py`, on a year for each of 256 seeds at the lighthouse's height every half hour, every row green:
  August's mean maximum 15.18 °C against 15.29 and its minimum 7.94 against 7.99, its daily range 7.24 against 7.30; the
  coldest half hour of August's mean day at 06:15, against sunrise at 06:35 on 16 August; August's rain 90.4 mm against
  89.9 and the year's 1,161.5 against 1,177.2; August's rain days 5.62 at 1 mm to 6.56 at 0.2 mm, round the lighthouse's
  6.0, and the year's 92.8 to 111.2, round 106.9.

**What stands on what.**

- *Measured:* the lighthouse's monthly means of its daily extremes, its rain and its rain days, 1991–2004, as Wikipedia
  reproduces the Bureau's table, whose rain days state no threshold.
- *Borrowed:* the warming since 1910, 1.51 ± 0.23 °C, and the cool season's drying since 1994, nine per cent, both State
  of the Climate 2024's and both for a whole region, not this coast; Parton and Logan's day, with constants measured for
  air at 1.5 m elsewhere; the standard atmosphere's lapse rate.
- *Designed:* the fronts as a sum of seeded waves; the cold snap's depth, v1's; the rain's threshold, scale and season,
  fitted to the lighthouse's months; the fronts' widening of a day's extremes, measured on the model itself.
- *Extrapolated:* a pre-human climate as today's record less the warming, with the drying undone in the same proportion
  month by month. Nothing here tests that; the check's arithmetic shows only that it was applied as stated.

**Not exercised, or not holding.**

- No observation the fits did not use: the Bureau's own tables, Nowra's record and any daily record are unread (DEBTS).
- Away from August the months keep only the record's broad shape. Holding August's 6.0 rain days, July and September
  come out with six or seven against 9.2 and 8.5. Each month's rain lies within about a fifth of the pre-human record's,
  but October's and December's are a third above it.
- The wind keeps v1's daily shape and the fronts' factor, and the cloud and the humidity v1's rules off the index; none
  is checked against a record.
- Among the tests' Augusts, one has no wet day and fifteen a single wet spell, and one dry run lasts three weeks: kept,
  as the weather's own, with the test holding most Augusts to fronts that come and go.
- The weather is a function of the seed, the clock and the build: a build that changes its settings gives a continued
  world other weather from the same instant, which nothing yet depends on.
