# Contract M1.2 — Engine world layers

**Status:** drafted 2026-09-08 while M1.B's corpus ran; opened 2026-09-09 on the owner's "continue work", with
the M1.B ruling (CANON ruling 11's decision) still his to enter. It does not depend on that decision: everything
here is engine-free computation over the baked raster, and it stands whichever way the netcode goes. Owner: Claude. The owner's lane: reading the census (promise
8) as a document about the country and saying where it reads wrong; no coding task (CANON ruling 17).

## What this slice promises

1. **The layer format, `eg2.raster` version 2.** Version 1 carries one f32 grid; version 2 adds the layer's name
   and its dtype (`u8`, `u16`, `i16`, `u32`, `f32`), a stated scale and unit for integer layers (soil depth in
   centimetres, wetness in 1/255), and the same checksum. `Tools/data/raster_io.py` writes it, `RegionRaster` reads
   it, and a version-1 file is read as a version-2 file whose layer is `heights` in metres; the loader refuses any
   other version. ARCHITECTURE §10 gains the row in the same commit as the writer.
2. **Drainage.** v1's `DrainageNetwork` ported (pit-fill, flow direction, catchment, the channel classes Damp,
   Trickle, Creek, Stream, Pond, and the constants with their referents) and run over the 2001 × 2001 raster in
   seconds, not minutes; `WaterTests` ported with it. The sea is a sink at the shore.
3. **Soil and stone.** v1's `SoilModel` (depth from curvature and repose, wetness by multiple-flow topographic
   wetness, texture and fertility from the stone) and `SurfaceGeology` with `GeologyScale`, finishing v1's open item
   E0: no flint in the Sydney Basin; silcrete, quartz, rhyolite beach pebbles and quartzite are what Bherwerre
   offers, each with its source in ECOSYSTEM.md. `SoilTests` and `GeologyTests` ported.
4. **Plant community.** v1's `PlantSpecies` and `PlantCommunity` (suitability product, dominance squared, overstory
   then understory) with the Bherwerre list: blackbutt and bangalay dune forest, Lomandra at the dune toe,
   grass-tree heath, the swamp and lake margins, the rock platforms bare. Each species row cites its source.
   `PlantTests` ported.
5. **Animal capacity and presence.** v1's `AnimalSpecies`, `AnimalCapacity` and `AnimalPresence` for the two species
   M1.7 will materialise (eastern grey kangaroo, a shorebird), so the tablet can already be asked about hours that
   have not happened. `AnimalTests` ported; the simulation of animals is M1.7's.
6. **Water bodies with the fresh flag, and a floor for the sea.** Lakes Windermere and McKenzie, Blacks Waterhole
   and Ryans Swamp are fresh; the bay and the ocean are salt. The sea's floor comes from the Geoscience Australia
   Jervis Bay bathymetry grids where they cover the box, else a stated depth-by-distance-from-shore rule, which
   pays DEBTS.md's "the sea has no floor" and lets wading begin; `region_stats.py` re-checks the sea's depth range.
7. **Topology.** A bitmask per cell: beach, dune, wetland, forest, heath, crest, cliff, shore platform, from the
   layers above and the slope; Steamers Head reads as cliff, Cave Beach as beach with dune behind it.
8. **The wake-point scorer and the census.** Cells scored against the criteria (fresh water within 500 m,
   knappable stone within 1 km, fibre and firewood within 500 m, shelter rock within 2 km); the winner is written
   to `world.json` as the wake, and `Region`'s stated wake becomes the census's expectation: the scorer must land
   within 300 m of the Cave Beach swale or say why it did not. The census is the same scorer read out as a
   document ("read country"): what lies within each distance of the wake, in words and metres.
9. **The world-creation pipeline and the world folder.** Creating a world runs the chain once from `heights` and
   writes every layer under `Saves/<world>/layers/` in the version-2 format, with `world.json` naming them and
   their checksums (ARCHITECTURE §6 amended in the same commit). The server reads the layers from the folder; a
   client still receives ground by streaming (M1.B), and the streaming of the other layers is M1.4's.
10. **Determinism.** Two creations of the same region are byte-identical in every layer file (`SimDeterminismTests`
    ported and extended to the chain), on this machine and stated as such: cross-platform bit-determinism is not
    promised (ARCHITECTURE §4.3).
11. **Two verifiers** (CANON ruling 17; independent of the tool by method): `drainage_check.py` recomputes flow
    accumulation with its own D8 in numpy over the raw raster and compares the catchment at the outlets of the
    two lakes and at the largest creek against the engine's layer, printing both; `census_check.py` reads the
    layers with numpy alone and checks the water layer at the published coordinates of Lake Windermere, Lake
    McKenzie and Ryans Swamp (fresh), the bay (salt), and the topology at Steamers Head (cliff) and Cave Beach
    (beach), naming each source.

## Non-goals

No rendering of any layer (M1.4 draws them); no animal behaviour (M1.7); no landform evolution (dormant by the
decision of 2026-09-07); no climate (its own contracted step); no streaming of the new layers to clients (M1.4);
no entity store (M1.3).

## How it is proved

- `dotnet test Engine/EarthGame.slnx`: exit 0, count as run, the ported suites by name.
- A world created from the command line (`EarthGame.ServerHost +server.world <dir>` or the shell's New world):
  the layer files listed with their sizes and checksums; the census printed; the run's time stated.
- `python Tools/verifiers/checks/drainage_check.py` and `census_check.py`: every row with both numbers; exit 0.
- `python Tools/gate/run_gate.py M1.2`: green (the gate gains the two verifiers).
- The owner reads the census and rules where it reads wrong; a wrong reading is a debt or a fix, never a
  reworded census.

## Exit

The closing commit carries the census as printed and the two verifiers' rows. What the census gets wrong about
the country, by the owner's reading, is recorded in DEBTS.md with its owner.
