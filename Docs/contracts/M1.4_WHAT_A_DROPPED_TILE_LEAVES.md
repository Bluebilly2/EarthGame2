# Contract M1.4 (f) — What a dropped tile leaves

**Status:** closed 2026-09-14, the exit record at the end; opened the same day, on William's "continue": the last of
the bug hunt's findings of 2026-09-13, and the one a walk across the region meets. Owner: Claude (Fable 5.1).

## What was found (2026-09-13)

- **A tile let go of leaves its ground and its water behind.** The client keeps at most 25 tiles of each layer and
  lets the farthest go as new ones arrive (M1.4b). Letting go destroys the tile's Terrain and its water's
  GameObject, but a Terrain's data and a mesh are objects Unity keeps until they are destroyed themselves, and
  neither is; so every kilometre walked past leaves a 513-post heightmap and a mesh in memory for the life of the
  process. A tile built again over an older copy of itself leaves the older copy's data the same way. Codex's
  probe of 2026-09-10 recorded both (`TERRAIN_DATA_SURVIVES`, `WATER_MESH_SURVIVES`).
- **A hole stays in the distant ground.** When a tile arrives, a hole is cut in the coarse region terrain under it
  so the two never show through each other; when the tile goes, the hole is left, and the coarse ground where the
  founder walked from shows nothing (`COARSE_HOLE_AFTER_EVICTION`).

## What this slice promises

1. **A tile let go of frees what it drew from:** its Terrain's data with the Terrain, and its water's mesh with the
   water; a tile built again over an older copy frees the older copy the same way; and leaving the world frees the
   coarse region and the far skirt.
2. **The coarse ground shows again where a tile stood** once the tile has gone: the hole cut under it is filled over
   exactly the cells the cut covered.
3. **One owner of freeing:** a Unity object the client made is freed through one helper, which frees at once in the
   editor's edit mode, where the tests run, and at the end of the frame in play.

## Non-goals

Tiles held under the founder, which are never let go; the memory the far layers hold, which are kept by design; a
measured memory curve over a long walk, which the corpus's memory-growth rows already bound.

## How it is proved

The edit-mode tests (`python Tools/unity/editmode.py`): a freed tile's data and a freed water tile's mesh are gone
at once, and the hole under a tile is filled once the tile has gone; the Unity-layer compile; `dotnet test`
unchanged. Codex's untracked probe, which drives the runtime's own letting-go in play mode and records what
survives, is read as a second witness where it runs. A walk in the built game long enough to let a tile go (more
than 25 tiles held) is not part of this proof (amended 2026-09-14, from a first draft that promised one): no
scenario walks that far, and the edit-mode tests hold the same calls the runtime makes.

## Exit record (2026-09-14)

**What exists.** `UnityObjects.Free`, the one way the client frees a Unity object it made: at the end of the frame in
play, at once in the editor's edit mode. `TerrainTileBuilder.Free` frees a tile's Terrain and its data,
`WaterTileBuilder.Free` a water tile and its mesh, and `GroundLayerBuilder.Free` now goes through the same helper.
`TerrainTileBuilder.FillHole` fills what `CutHole` cut, the two sharing one rounding. `ClientRuntime` frees a tile let
go of and its water, fills the coarse ground under it, frees an older copy of a tile before building it again, and on
leaving the world frees every tile, water and colour it holds with the coarse region and the skirt; the coarse
terrain's own position is now the origin both the cut and the fill are asked from. What a player notices: on a long
walk the game no longer grows by a tile's ground and water for every kilometre passed, and the far ground behind them
no longer has holes where the tiles they left stood; neither was seen by eye in this slice.

**Tested, and how.**

- Edit-mode: 11 of 11, three of them new. A freed tile's data is gone at once and a freed water tile's mesh with it,
  and the hole cut under a 10 m tile in a 40 m coarse tile is filled by the same call's rounding, nowhere else touched.
- Codex's untracked probe, which drives `ClientRuntime`'s own letting-go in play mode over a frame and records what
  survives, read after the run: `COARSE_HOLE_AFTER_EVICTION=False`, `TERRAIN_DATA_SURVIVES=False`,
  `WATER_MESH_SURVIVES=False`, where its record of 2026-09-10 had all three `True`. Its first run against this slice
  threw a null reference: it gives the runtime a coarse terrain and no region, and the fill read the coarse origin
  from the region; the origin is now read from the coarse terrain itself, for the cut as for the fill.
- The Unity-layer compile: 0 warnings, 0 errors. `dotnet test` unchanged by this slice: nothing engine-side changed.

**Not exercised, or not holding.**

- No walk in the built game let a tile go, and no frame shows the far ground behind a walk; the memory freed was not
  measured in the process. The corpus's memory-growth rows will show it on their next full run.
- The far layers, kept by design, still hold what they hold; the sea plane and the probe objects are not freed on
  leaving the world, which today only happens by quitting.
