## What was found (2026-09-13)

- **An animal stood up is placed, not moved.** Once a second M1.7a's stand-up puts every member where presence has
  its group now. Nothing is remembered between seconds, so an animal cannot yet be anywhere presence does not put it,
  and presence's own comment says why: an animal that fled would be state.
- **The server knows where every founder is, every tick.** Each session's body is the server's to validate, and the
  stand-up already reads the founders' places.
- **A running animal would jump.** The client draws an entity three ticks behind the newest state it holds
  (`GameClient.MirrorDelayTicks`), between the places it was told. That is smooth for things told every tick and a
  stop-start for animals told once a second: a kangaroo bounding at 7 m/s would jump 7 m once a second.
- **A pose travels.** A state carrying an animal's pose goes reliably since M1.7a; the reader takes any pose byte, so a
  new pose needs no new field, only a new meaning (a protocol version).
- **What is published about how close a person gets.**
  - Kangaroos: red kangaroos and euros in the rangelands took flight from a person walking up at 78 m on a track and
    90 m off it, fled from a person on foot three times in four and from a car two times in five, and let people
    closer in the evening, in cover and on still days (Wolf and Croft 2010, *Applied Animal Behaviour Science* 126:
    75–84). Eastern greys learn: they soon stop fleeing early from people who do them no harm, and where people shoot
    them they wait until the last moment before bolting (Austin and Ramp 2019, *Ecology and Evolution*; a walker got
    16 m closer after the first alert where people were many and harmless, and 2 m where they were many and harmful).
  - The pied oystercatcher takes flight from a person at 39 to 83 m, and at 129 m from a person with a dog (Weston and
    others 2012, *Emu* 112; Harrison 2009; both as cited by the 2020 study of the species on the beaches of eastern
    Australia). A roosting Eurasian oystercatcher disturbed moved about 200 m before settling (*Avian Research*, 2019).
  - Animals cut off from their predators grow tame: island lizards flee later than mainland ones (Cooper, Pyron and
    Garland 2014, *Proceedings of the Royal Society B*). But wallabies isolated on islands were less watchful and no
    less quick to run than mainland ones (Blumstein and Daniel 2005, the same journal). Before people, Bherwerre's
    kangaroos had predators of their own; what they had never met is a person.
- **How fast they go.**
  - Kangaroos hopping over force plates in a zoo's enclosure chose 2.0 to 4.5 m/s, and would not be made to hop faster
    there (a 2025 study in *eLife* of sixteen red and grey kangaroos of 14 to 27 kg). Guinness World Records lists
    64 km/h for a mature female eastern grey and 56 km/h held over 1.6 km by a large male red, with no date, place or
    method given.
  - Birds: across 138 species, measured airspeeds run from 8 to 23 m/s, and shorebirds are among the fast flyers at
    15 to 20 m/s (Alerstam and others 2007, *PLOS Biology*).
- **A founder's pace.** On flat ground a founder walks at 1.8 m/s and runs at 4.7 m/s (`Locomotion`). No founder runs a
  kangaroo down.

## What this slice promises

1. **Noticing.** Every step, a standing group takes flight when a founder comes within its kind's flight distance of any
   of its members.
2. **Fleeing.** A group that takes flight runs straight away from the founder that startled it, its members keeping their
   places round it: a mob of kangaroos bounding at 7 m/s for about 150 m, a pair of oystercatchers flying off at 15 m/s
   for about 200 m. It keeps to dry ground inside the region, turning along a shore or the region's edge rather than
   into it, and stops where it cannot turn. While it flees its members are moved every step, so a client draws them
   running, not jumping; the rest stay once a second.
3. **Settling, and going back.** A group that has fled stands and grazes, then walks back at a walking pace to where
   presence puts it, and from there presence has it again. A founder who comes near again sends it off again.
4. **The server's, and forgotten.** What a group is doing is held by the stand-up while it stands, and forgotten when it
   is taken away; stood up again, it is where presence puts it. Nothing is saved or named by the world's digest.
5. **On the wire (protocol 12; 10 when this was written, before M1.D took 10 and 11).** An animal's pose gains a third
   meaning, fleeing (running or flying), and a Hello of another version is refused as ever. Two players watching see
   the same flight.
6. **Checked.** The host records every flight when it begins: the group, its kind, the founder, how far off they were
   and which way it ran. `fauna_check` holds every flight to its kind's distance, both numbers printed, and holds every
   place logged while fleeing to dry ground, as it holds every other.
7. **Within the budgets.** The corpus, N1 to N4, green with the players startling animals round the loop; each client
   under 20 KB a second on average after its join; what flights cost an update read from the host's own records.
8. **Tests.** A group takes flight within its distance and not beyond it; runs away from the founder, at its speed, for
   its length, on dry ground; settles, goes back, and is presence's again; is sent off again by a founder who follows;
   is forgotten when taken away; is moved every step only while it flees; and two clients see the same flight, the
   mirror naming what the server names once it has stopped.
9. **The rules as the panel's sliders** (added 2026-09-14, CANON ruling 30). How near a founder sends each kind
   running, how far it runs and how fast are rows of the developer's table (`DevSettings`, M1.D), moved by a
   development server's settings and never saved; the numbers in `AnimalFlightRules` are the defaults.

## Non-goals

Hunting, catching, wounding or touching an animal. An animal that charges or fights back. Stealth: crouching, the wind,
cover and the hour changing how close a founder gets (Wolf and Croft's evenings, cover and still days), and animals
learning a founder is harmless (Austin and Ramp). A mob that scatters. A bird that lands on the water. Sounds. The
drawing of a running kangaroo or a flying bird (M1.7b).

## Owed a source

- **The speeds are design inside the measured bounds, not measurements.** A mob's 7 m/s (25 km/h) sits between what
  kangaroos chose in an enclosure and the records; the oystercatcher's 15 m/s is the floor of the shorebirds' band.
  The study's own row for the oystercatcher is in its supplement, which could not be read here without installing a
  PDF reader.
- **How far they run.** A mob's 150 m rests on nothing yet read: Wolf and Croft measured flight lengths, and their
  paper is to be read before this slice closes. The oystercatcher's 200 m is the Eurasian species' at a roost.
