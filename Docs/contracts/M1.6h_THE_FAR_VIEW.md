**Status:** drafted 2026-09-25 after William's play, to be built after the height re-bake (BF.4's holes) and before the
stone of the place (BF.4 promise 6). Owner: Claude (the main session). It pays DEBTS "The far view is not the country".

## Amended 2026-09-25 under ruling 50

Ruling 50 makes the world a planet, made as it is visited, drawn in levels of detail to the horizon in its second stage.
Promises 2 and 3 are dropped: painting the surround from NVIS's groups and drawing the far forest as a canopy are what
that stage does properly, over the whole planet and not one skirt, and the NVIS service carries into it unfetched. What is
built is promises 1 and 4, the parts that carry into the planet whatever it draws:

1. **The colours, one conversion.** `Rgb.ToLinear` in ClientCore decodes a table colour by the sRGB standard's own curve
   (IEC 61966-2-1), and every colour a stand mesh or an animal's skin carries comes through it: `StandMeshes.ToColor` for
   the crowns, trunks, limbs, far trees, tufts, sticks, strips, cords, logs, cobbles, rocks and the stripped trunks'
   sapwood (now a table colour, `StandForms.Sapwood`), and `AnimalLooks` for the skins. `LinearColor`, the rocks' own
   copy, is gone. A face's jitter scales the colour once linear, as it always scaled what the shader took, so the faces
   stray from one another as much as they were seen to; a crown's green nudge is of the table's value, before the
   conversion, which keeps it within a fifth of how strong it was seen. The tables' values are unchanged.
2. **The haze and the far plane.** The pipeline's fog is off. Every shader that draws the land, what stands on it and the
   water fades what it draws by one function (`Haze.hlsl`): light reaches the eye by exp(−τ), τ the aerosol's optical
   depth along the line, the aerosol densest at the sea and thinning with height over 1.2 km (Bruneton's reference
   implementation of precomputed atmospheric scattering), integrated exactly along the line; the haze's own light is the
   sky's colour at the horizon in that direction, read from a cube of 128 texels a side the sky is rendered into whenever
   the sun has moved a quarter of a degree. Its depth is the weather's: 40 km of visibility at the lighthouse's usual
   humidity of 70 % (the WMO scale's very clear air, 20 to 50 km, stated, since neither station's record gives one), deepening
   with the humidity as the aerosol's scattering does, f(RH) = a(1 − RH)^−0.6 (Titos et al. 2021's moderately hygroscopic
   aerosol), from about 61 km in dry air to 10 km at 97 %. The land the client holds fades wholly into the haze over the
   last eighth of its half-width (the surround's), and below the horizon beyond it a bowl round the eye is drawn in the
   haze's colour, where the procedural sky showed its dark ground; so from any height what the game holds ends in the
   air. The bowl is the air, not land: the coarser ring of land for high flights stays unpromised, and is the planet's
   second stage's. The far plane is the air's, just past the bowl, which stands at twice the surround's half-diagonal
   (181 km for the whole valley, 90 km for Bherwerre). The camera's depth is URP's default attachment, 32-bit float,
   reversed (`CoreUtils.GetDefaultDepthStencilFormat` on Windows), whose steps stay a fraction of a metre at that range.

How it is proved, as amended: `TableColourTests` (the decoding against the standard's values) and `HazeTests` (the
visibility against the law worked by hand) in the fast suite; the edit-mode `EveryColourDrawnIsItsTablesInLinearLight`
(every corner of every stand mesh held to the way its builder makes it from its tables, made linear by Unity's own
decoding) and `EveryAnimalsSkinIsItsTablesInLinearLight`, each sabotaged by putting one site back to the table's sRGB;
frames before and after at Bherwerre's wake and Windermere (the near forest, the understorey, and the far forest from
150 m up), its wake from 1.5 km up, the whole valley's escarpment and village from 1.5 km up and its escarpment from 4 km
up, for his eyes; and a held turn of 20 s at Bherwerre's wake and the whole valley's escarpment before and after, for the
frame's cost. The recorder's lookout climbs at the flight's running pace until 300 m short of the height asked for, so
those heights are reached inside its time.

## What was found before it was designed (2026-09-25)

William flew 1.7 km up over the whole Kangaroo Valley on the rocks build: "i see the valley now, but the world doesnt
generate properly that far". Two things were wrong, and neither is the land's shape, which is the bake's to 64 km out:

- **Everything the stand draws is paler than its table.** The stand shader takes a vertex colour as linear light, and every
  colour in `StandForms` is written in sRGB as the ground palette is; `StandMeshes.ToColor` passes them through unchanged.
  The rocks drew nearly white until they were made linear that morning (065c0ae), and the frames beside the escarpment's
  wake still show white cobbles. The far trees, one kept crown spread wide for many (M1.6f), read as pale blobs.
- **The land past the region is one flat tan.** The surround (the whole valley's is 128 km at 64 m) is drawn as a skirt with
  its true relief, but it wears the ground layer and nothing else, since no cover travels past the region. From the
  escarpment the country beyond 16 km is a desert.

- **Past that, nothing.** From 4.2 km up that afternoon he saw the four bands in turn ("there is still a rectangle with the
  near things ... past that there is a lower quality render with weird green blobs of slightly varying shades, then a tan
  section that looks like it has almost nothing. then after than there is nothing, the game doesnt seem to render anything
  at all"): the camera draws to 40 km (`ClientRuntime`'s far plane), the whole valley's surround reaches 64 km and the
  horizon from 4 km up is over 200 km off, and nothing in the game thins the air with distance, so the land stops at a line.

The valley is a place of views from its escarpment and its lookouts, so the far view is much of its character.

## What this slice promises

1. **Every stand colour made linear, in one place.** `StandMeshes` converts every table colour as it builds a mesh, the
   crowns, the trunks, the far trees, the tufts, the sticks and the cobbles as the rocks already are, and `ToColor` goes.
   Frames at the vantages of both places before and after, 1440p and 1080p, for William's eyes: the near forest, the
   understorey and the far forest all change, and his eyes decide whether the tables themselves want retuning.
2. **The land past the region wears its vegetation before 1750.** Australia's own reconstruction of what grew before the
   clearing: NVIS 7.0's pre-1750 Major Vegetation Groups (DCCEEW, CC BY 4.0, 100 m). Fetched for each region's surround box
   as images from the Department's map service (`ogc_services/NVIS_pre_mvg`), decoded by its legend's 31 colours, and baked
   as `vegetation.u8` on the surround's grid, the group names its legend. The skirt's picture colours each square by its
   group: a forest group its foliage over the forest floor by a crown share stated per group (tall open forest dense,
   woodland open, rainforest closed), heath, shrubland and grassland their own colours, fresh water and the sea theirs, bare
   rock and sand the ground's. Made once when the region loads, as the far canopy's picture is (M1.6g); nothing on the wire.
3. **The far forest, judged after its colours.** Promise 1 may be enough. If the frames from 1.5 km up still show separate
   blobs where the ground below is forest, the kept crowns are drawn as the canopy they stand for, spread in height as they
   are in plan. That is decided on the frames and recorded here.

4. **The edge dissolves into the air.** Aerial haze: the far land fades toward the sky's own colour at the horizon with
   distance, as clear air does (visibility of tens of kilometres, the haze's depth taken from the weather's humidity where
   the weather says it), so what the game holds ends in haze rather than at a line; the camera's far plane reaches the whole
   surround. A coarser ring of land out to the horizon for high flights is weighed on the frames and not promised.

## Non-goals

No trees are planted past the region: the skirt is coloured, not stood on. The region's own plants are not changed. Today's
farms and towns are not drawn; the source is the vegetation before them (GAME_DESIGN section 2: this Earth has no dams).

## How it is proved

- Tests first: an edit-mode test that every mesh's vertex colour is its table colour made linear (sabotaged by putting one
  `ToColor` back); `FarSkirt`'s tests in ClientCore (a square of each kind of group gets its colour, a square of no data the
  ground's, rows south to north as the far canopy's).
- `vegetation_check.py`, independent of the bake: it asks the same service's `identify` for the group at sampled points of
  each region's box, a path that never goes through the exported colours, and holds the baked layer to it point for point,
  printing both names beside the verdict. As a lead and not a gate, it also counts how much of the region's own forest (our
  plant model's overstory) stands where NVIS has a forest group.
- The frame's cost unchanged: a held turn at the whole valley's escarpment with the skirt coloured and without.
- Frames from the escarpment's lookouts (Cambewarra, Fitzroy Falls), from 1.5 km up over the village and at Bherwerre, for
  his eyes. The seam at the region's edge, where the far canopy's own picture meets NVIS's, is one of the things he judges.

## Data

Two or three image exports per region box from the service, a few megabytes in all, under `Data/cache/nvis/` with a
`fetch_nvis.py` that writes each file's size and SHA-256 beside it (CANON 49: downloads are the agents' to make, each
recorded). `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` gains NVIS with the Department's attribution. The whole-country file (112.5 MB, a File
Geodatabase) is not needed, and reading it would want GDAL, which the tools do not carry.
