# M1.5h — The walk, given a law of its own

**Status:** built 2026-09-21, his hands owed; drafted 2026-09-21 on CANON ruling 34 ("The walk is not good enough"), next in the order given to William
that day: after M1.E and the ripples, before the understorey and the footsteps (ruling 37). Owner: Claude. The
decision is his hands' (ruling 12): the flat walk he passed stands, the slopes and the feel are what this slice answers.

**The one sentence:** Tobler's hiking function is fitted to journey times over hours, pauses and route-finding
included, and the port uses it as the founder's speed second by second, so a founder walks down a dune slower than a
stroll and every change of speed is instant; this slice gives the player's own motion a law of its own, taken from
what people are measured doing when they walk on slopes, and keeps Tobler for what it was fitted to.

## What is wrong now, and why

- `Locomotion.SpeedMs` (`Engine/packages/com.earthgame.engine/Runtime/Body/Locomotion.cs`) is Tobler's function
  times a travel-pace factor of 1.3 (a correction invented to turn an hour-average into a stepping pace), times the
  gait, times the body's capacity, floored at 0.45 m/s. Tobler is symmetric about a −5 % grade, so a 25–30° descent
  returns about 0.3 m/s and lands on the floor: the dune behind the wake is descended at a crawl (William, 2026-09-20).
- `Mover.Step` (`Runtime/Player/Mover.cs`) sets the horizontal velocity to the wish times that speed in one tick,
  and to nothing the tick the keys are let go: no acceleration, no braking, no turning of the velocity. v1's slice
  had 4 m/s² of ground acceleration and about 1.5 m of stopping (`Docs/v1/SLICE2_7_LOCOMOTION.md`, "Movement that
  feels like a body"); the port kept Tobler and dropped those, and ruling 18 stood on gentle ground only.
- `MoverConfig.WalkableSlopeDeg` is 45°: a face steeper than anything loose can stand at is "walked" at the floor
  speed, when a body on it would be sliding or climbing.
- The stride (M1.5c, `Stride.cs`) and the server's `MovementValidator` both take their pace from the same
  `Locomotion`, which is right and must stay so: one law, three readers.

## What people are measured doing (the sources this slice stands on)

| Source | What it measured | What it gives this slice |

| Sun, Walters, Svensson & Lloyd 1996, *Ergonomics* 39:677–692 | 2 400 pedestrians on a ramp of up to 9° at Circular Quay, Sydney | The step shortens on a descent; uphill the cadence falls and the stride lengthens. A gentle slope does not slow a walker |
| Kawamura, Tokuhiro & Takechi 1991, *Acta Med Okayama* 45:179–184 | 17 men on 3°, 6°, 9° and 12° slopes | At 12° the speed is significantly lower both up and down; the steeper the descent the shorter the step. The speed falls with the step, not with a curve fitted to hours |
| Effects of hiking-dependent walking speeds and slopes on spatiotemporal gait parameters, *Applied Sciences* 14 (2024) 4383 | 32 adults on an instrumented treadmill at 0, ±10 and ±20 % | The hiking speeds the study set for those grades: 5.0 km/h level, 3.5 at +10 %, 2.5 at +20 %, 5.0 at −10 %, 3.5 at −20 % — the anchor table for the walk |
| Analysis of sloped gait: how many steps to steady-state speed after gait initiation, *Gait & Posture* 83 (2021) | Gait initiation on level, uphill and downhill | 90 % (uphill) and 95 % (downhill) of the steady speed by the third step, mostly by cadence: a body is at its pace in about a second |

## Promises

1. **A speed law for stepping.** `Locomotion` gains the walker's own speed against the slope along the motion, a
   table with the sources' points and straight lines between them: 1.39 m/s on the level and on a gentle descent
   (to −10 %), 0.97 m/s at −20 % and at +10 %, 0.69 m/s at +20 %; steeper, the speed falls as the step shortens
   (Kawamura's trend continued) to a careful pace at 24° either way and to the limit of walked ground at 35°. No
   travel-pace factor: the numbers are stepping speeds already. The gait multipliers and the body's capacity apply as
   today. Tobler stays as `Locomotion.JourneySpeedMs`, read by nothing on the mover's path.
2. **A body reaches its pace, and stops in its own length.** The mover's horizontal velocity moves toward the wished
   velocity at a rate that reaches a walk from rest in about a second (the third step) and a run in about the same
   count of steps; let go, it brakes at four metres a second per second (v1's figure): a quarter of a metre from a
   walk, a metre and a half from a run. A reversed wish turns the velocity through the stop, never in one tick. In the
   air the control stays as it is.
3. **Walked ground ends where loose ground does.** `WalkableSlopeDeg` becomes 35°, the dune's repose; steeper than
   that the founder slides as the mover already lets them, with no control, and the validator agrees. The slide is
   not made a mechanic here.
4. **One law, three readers.** The mover, the server's `MovementValidator` and the client's stride all take their
   pace from the one `Locomotion`; the corpus's walkers walk the new law on the loop, whose steepest segment is 28°.
5. **Nothing else moves.** Swimming, wading factors, crouching, jumping, the flight, the cost of moving (Pandolf with
   the actual speed) and the thirst's pricing are untouched; the flat walk's speed changes from 1.57 to about 1.39 m/s
   at the desk, which is the one flat number this slice moves, and it moves toward the sources.

## Non-goals

Stamina apart from the energy budget; a load that slows as well as costs; the slide as a controlled descent;
climbing with hands; stairs; the gamepad's stick as a speed (it is one already); any change to the run's multiple.

## How it is proved

- **Headless tests, written first:** the table returns the sources' points at the sources' grades; a 30° descent is
  faster than today's floor and slower than the flat; the speed at 35° is the last walked speed and 36° is a slide;
  from rest the mover is at 90 % of the walk within 1.5 s and at 90 % of the run within about a second; let go at a
  walk it stops within half a metre and from a run within two and a half; a reversed wish passes through a stop; the validator accepts what the mover does
  on every segment of the corpus's loop and refuses a speed above the law. Each rule broken once and seen red.
- **The controls scenario** on the built player: the walk and the run at the desk and on the pad still pass their
  checks with the new numbers.
- **The corpus's walk and walk-solo** on the loop, `join_check --only N2` green, no correction storm.
- **A dune recorded:** the controls scenario gains a descent of the dune behind the wake with the speed and the slope
  in its record, so the number he felt is written beside what he feels.
- **His hands** (ruling 12, DEBTS' row of 2026-09-20): ten minutes on the dune and a bank.

## Exit record (2026-09-21)

**What exists.** `Locomotion.WalkingSpeedMs` is a table of the speeds people are measured walking at on slopes, straight
lines between its points and the ends held past them: 1.39 m/s on the level and to −10 %, 0.97 at −20 % and at +10 %,
0.69 at +20 %, 0.69 on a 24° descent (the slowest trial Minetti's subjects walked at every gradient), 0.50 on a 24°
ascent and 0.40 and 0.35 at 35° (the slice's own continuation of Kawamura's trend, marked for his hands). `SpeedMs` is
that times the gait and the body's capacity, with no travel-pace factor and no floor. Tobler stays as
`Locomotion.JourneySpeedMs`, read by nothing on the mover's path; Pandolf's cost takes the actual speed as before.
`Mover.Step` moves the horizontal velocity toward the wished one at the gait's acceleration (`WalkAccelMs2` 1.05,
`RunAccelMs2` 3.2) and at the brake (`BrakeMs2` 4.0) when it slows or turns, as a vector, so a reversed wish passes through
a stop. `MoverConfig.WalkableSlopeDeg` is 35°, dry sand's repose; the validator's ceiling is the table's fastest point
times the run's multiple (`FastestWalkSlope`). The `dune` scenario (`Recorder.Dune.cs`, `Tools/world/dune.py`) finds the
steepest face of 18° to 34° within 150 m of the founder on the client's own ground, walks down it and up it by script,
and writes the slope walked beside the speed held. What a player notices: the flat walk a little slower (1.39 for 1.57),
a start that takes a step or two, a stop that takes a stride, and a dune walked down at a careful pace instead of a crawl.
