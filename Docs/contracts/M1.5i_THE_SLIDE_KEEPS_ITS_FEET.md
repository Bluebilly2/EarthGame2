# M1.5i — The slide keeps its feet

**Status:** opened and built 2026-09-21, the evening M1.5h landed, on William's report while playing it: "i walked up to a
slope and fell through the world, it was quite steep and i was attempting to walk up the hill." Owner: Claude. The fix
that lets M1.5h's promise 3 stand: walked ground ends at dry sand's repose, 35°.

**The one sentence:** a founder on ground too steep to stand on is not grounded, so nothing holds their feet up; the
client's sweep is a PhysX capsule cast that starts with its foot already inside the sloping terrain and reports nothing;
gravity takes the body through the world. The mover keeps a sliding body's feet on the surface itself, whatever the sweep
fails to see.

## What was wrong

- M1.5h set `MoverConfig.WalkableSlopeDeg` to 35°, so a face between 35° and 45°, walked the day before, became a slide.
  The slide had been exercised on the engine's heightfield collision only (the 61° sliding test), which is analytic and
  exact.
- On the client (`Unity/Assets/EarthGame/Client/PhysxCollision.cs`) the sweep is `Physics.CapsuleCast`, whose capsule's foot
  sphere overlaps a steep face at the start of every step; a cast that starts inside a collider reports no hit, so the
  sweep said the way was free, the body moved by its whole fall, and the next step began further inside.
- The limit went back to 45° for the evening (commit 3f9e54c), which is where the face he fell through was walked, not
  slid, and the debt was written with the mechanism.

## Promises

1. **A sliding body keeps its feet on the surface.** In `Mover.Step`, a falling body whose feet the sweep has let under
   the ground under them is put back on that surface and the part of its velocity into the surface is taken away, so what
   is left is the slide down it. The rule lives in the engine, once, for the client and the server alike.
2. **The blind spot is in the tests.** A collision that wraps the exact heightfield and ignores any sweep whose capsule
   starts inside the ground, the way the client's does, lets a body through the face before the fix and not after.
3. **Walked ground ends at the repose again.** `WalkableSlopeDeg` is 35°; W4 and the mover's face test say so.
4. **Proved on the built player.** The dune scenario walks into the steepest face too steep to stand on within 300 m of
   the founder, from below, and records the lowest the feet were read below the ground; over five centimetres is through
   the world.

## Non-goals

A controlled slide; the server's below-the-ground correction, which is asked separately why it did not catch him; any
change to the client's collision itself.

## How it is proved

- **Headless, red first:** on a 50° face with the blind sweep the body went 21 m through the face in two seconds; with
  the fix it never goes more than five centimetres under and slides down. The lift broken once (never firing) and the
  test seen red; the file put back byte for byte.
- **The dune scenario** on the built player: the dune walked as before, and the face over the limit walked into with the
  feet read against the ground every frame.
- **The controls scenario** both ways and the corpus's quick walk, unchanged and green.

## Exit record (2026-09-21)

**What exists.** In `Mover.Step`, a falling body whose feet the sweep has let under the ground under them (a probe a step's
height up, none down) is put back on that surface and the part of its velocity into the surface is taken away; what is
left is the slide down it. `MoverConfig.WalkableSlopeDeg` is 35° again. The dune scenario's third part looks for the
steepest face over the limit within 300 m, walks to three metres below it and onto it, and reads the feet against the
ground every frame, with the steepest ground under them and how far short of the face's foot the walk stopped.
