# Contract M1.4c — The water is drawn

**Status:** opened 2026-09-10 after M1.4b closed. Owner: Claude (Opus 5). The owner's lane: the frames of promise
6, which is the first time a lake of this world has been drawn at all, and his eyes decide whether it reads as
water lying in the country rather than a blue lid on it.

M1.4b told the client where water stands and drew none of it. This slice draws the standing water it holds.

## What this slice promises

1. **Drawn from what the client was sent, at the height it puts back together.** A tile's water surface is its
   ground plus the depth streamed over it (M1.4b), and the mesh sits at that height. Nothing reads the world
   folder on the client's side, so what is drawn is what was sent, and a tile with no depth draws nothing.
2. **A cell is water when all four of its posts carry depth**, so the drawn edge stops at the last cell wholly
   under water and never laps over dry ground. Its height is the lowest of its four posts' surfaces, so an edge
   cell never stands above its own bank.
   *Superseded 2026-09-20 by M1.4g:* a cell with dry corners is drawn too, at its highest wet corner's surface
   held down to the lowest dry corner's ground, because this promise as written hid every creek in the world —
   WG.1's channels are one cell wide and no cell along them has four wet corners. What this promise was for, that
   water never laps over dry ground, is kept by the clamp and proved by its own test.
3. **A quad per run, not per cell.** Wet cells along a row are merged into one quad, broken where the surface
   height changes, because standing water is flat: a body a hundred cells wide costs one quad a row instead of a
   hundred. The run-finding is engine-free in `EarthGame.ClientCore` and tested headlessly; Unity turns runs into
   a mesh and nothing else.
4. **The sea keeps the plane it has had since 2026-09-08**, and tile water is drawn only where the surface stands
   above the datum. The two therefore never contend for one surface, and the sea beyond the streamed tiles is
   still drawn. That the sea is not yet drawn from the layer that knows its shape is stated here and recorded in
   DEBTS.md rather than implied.
5. **Water lives and dies with its ground tile.** The mesh is built when a tile's depth arrives and destroyed
   when the client lets that tile go (M1.4b's residency), so what is drawn is what is held.
6. **Frames of Lake Windermere**, 21.0 ha standing at 16.2 m and up to 4.5 m deep in the gate world, at 1440p
   and 1080p, recorded windowless and muted from the built player. The founder cannot walk there inside a
   recording — the lake is 1,863 m from this world's wake — so `EarthGame.ServerHost` gains a console command
   `stand <name> <east> <north>` that puts a remembered body where the thing being judged is. It is a
   diagnostic, like `spawn`, and it says what it did.
7. **Tests:** the run-finder against a made tile — a full row merging to one quad, a dry row to none, an edge
   taking the lowest of its posts, two levels never merging across their boundary — and the whole path from a
   streamed depth tile to the quads a view would build.

## Non-goals

The sea drawn from the layer (promise 4 and its debt). Creeks, streams and swamps, which this world gives no
depth and which are the ground's own colour until the oracle draws them (M1.6). Waves, reflection, refraction,
transparency tuned by depth, shorelines, foam: the material is the one the sea already uses, and the look is
judged as a whole later. Wading, which changes what the server accepts and waits for the M1.B ruling.

## What changed on the way

- **The water mesh is built once per pair, not once per layer.** Either layer arriving asks for the pair, so a
  join built every mesh twice; the two checksums together now name what a mesh was built from. Four builds
  became two for two tiles.
- **Promise 7's edge test asserted the wrong thing at first.** It expected an edge cell not to merge with its
  neighbour, when the neighbour legitimately shares the same low post and merging is right. The test now checks
  the promise itself: no quad stands above a post it covers.
