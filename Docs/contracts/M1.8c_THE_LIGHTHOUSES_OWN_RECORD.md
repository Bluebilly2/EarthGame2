# Contract M1.8c — The lighthouse's own record

**Status:** opened and closed 2026-09-16 (exit record below), the day William pasted the Bureau of Meteorology's tables
for Point Perpendicular Lighthouse (068034) and Nowra RAN Air Station AWS (068072) into the conversation, the two pages
M1.8a and M1.7a had owed since 2026-09-13 ("station normals are hand-pulled", DEBTS). Owner: Claude (Fable 5.1). What the weather stood in
for until a record was read is replaced by the record: the station's months, the rain days' threshold, the wind's shape,
the cloud, the humidity and the spread of the nights.

## What the tables say (Data/stations/, the source; every figure below is theirs)

- **The months are milder by day and the same by night.** The 1907–2004 means of the daily maximum run 23.8 °C in January
  to 15.1 in July and 16.1 in August; the minimum 17.5 to 9.2 and 9.6. M1.8a's 1991–2004 reproduction had August's day at
  16.8: a short, warm record. Ninety-five years of it stand 0.61 °C above the pre-human coast by the warming trend's value
  at their middle year (1955), where M1.8a took the whole 1.51 °C off fourteen recent years: the pre-human August night
  comes out at 9.0 °C, a degree milder than M1.8a's 8.0.
- **The rain falls on more days than the reproduction said, evenly through the year.** 1,241.8 mm on 136.5 days of rain
  (0.2 mm or more), 110.5 of 1 mm or more, 34.0 of 10 mm or more, 1899–2004. August has 8.3 days of 1 mm or more where
  the reproduction, with no threshold stated, had 6.0; the months run from 8.3 to 10.2, so the year's fronts come almost
  as often in late winter as in autumn. M1.8a bent its season to the 6.0 and left July and September short, as its
  contract suspected.
- **The wind blows harder than v1's shape, and the morning as hard as the afternoon in winter.** 9 am means 13.5 to 19.1
  km/h at 10 m by month, 3 pm 17.9 to 22.5; the year 15.8 and 20.0. v1's day peaked at noon at 4 m/s and fell to 1.5 at
  night, a third low in the afternoon. Nowra's airfield, 29 km inland at 109 m, gives 14.3 and 20.0 for the year: the
  lighthouse's wind is the region's open wind, not a headland's alone.
- **The sky is about half covered, more in summer.** 9 am cloud 5.0 oktas in January to 3.7 in August; 3 pm 4.6 to 3.8.
- **Mornings are damp and afternoons dry with the same moisture in the air.** 9 am humidity 69–77 %, 3 pm 61–72 %; the
  dew points at 9 am and 3 pm lie within half a degree of each other in every month (16.2/16.8 in January, 6.6/6.9 in
  July).
- **August's nights spread 5.2 °C from the first decile to the ninth** (7.0 to 12.2, 1946–2004), its days 6.1 (13.3 to
  19.4). The coldest August reading in 57 years was −0.4 °C, the coldest of any month −0.5.
- **The sun's energy on the ground**, the Bureau's satellite figure for the site: 22.9 MJ/m² a day in January, 8.1 in
  June, 12.6 in August.
- **Nowra, inland**, is colder by night and warmer by day: July's mean minimum 6.8 against the lighthouse's 9.2, its
  maximum 16.8 against 15.1; the coldest reading −0.9; 1,029.5 mm on 86.5 days of 1 mm or more. The region lies between
  the two: no point of it is 29 km from the sea.

## What this slice promises

1. **The climate stands on the station's own table.** `Climate`'s months are the Bureau's 1907–2004 means; the warming
   taken off a table is the trend's value at the middle of the table's years (`Climate.WarmingInsideRecordC`), 0.61 °C for
   this one, stated as a rule so any later table gets the same treatment. Every month within a degree of the table less
   that; August within a third.
2. **The rain re-solved to the long record**, by a tool that calls the engine's own formulas with candidates (the almanac's
   `+fit rain`; the constants in `Synoptic` are what it printed): the months' rain, days of 1 mm or more, days of any rain
   and days of 10 mm or more together. Held: August's and the year's rain within a tenth of the pre-human totals (the
   drying undone for the eleven of the record's hundred and six years inside it); the rain days at 1 mm and at any rain
   within a tenth for August and the year, which the stated thresholds now allow; the heavy days within a day a month and
   a fifth a year; the cool half of the year raining more and harder as before.
3. **The wind is the record's.** `Climate.WindSpeedMs` takes the day of the year: the station's 9 am and 3 pm means by
   month, brought from 10 m to a founder's 1.5 m by the logarithmic profile over grass (`BodyHeightWindShare`), read
   through the day as a night floor and a half-sine rise from six to a peak at three, which the two readings fix; the
   fronts' factor reads the frontal band alone, its mean a hundredth over one. Held at nine and at three, every month,
   within a twelfth.
4. **The cloud has its own year**, fitted to the oktas (`+fit cloud`): a level and a yearly wave with the index on top.
   Held at nine and at three, every month, within half an okta.
5. **The humidity comes from a dew point.** The station's month, shifted by the cold snap and by the index, read against
   the air's temperature by the Magnus form (`Climate.SaturationVapourKPa`, now also the breath's one owner). Held at
   nine and at three, every month, within eight points (the check holds August and January within six); mornings damper
   than afternoons. The model runs three to seven points dry, worst in November's afternoon, for the reason the exit
   record names: the day's curve is a degree warm at those hours.
6. **The nights spread as the deciles say.** The cold snap's depth (`Synoptic.ColdSnapDepthC`) is set by `+fit depth` so
   August's daily minima over many seeds run 5.2 °C from the first decile to the ninth, and the fronts' widening of the
   day's extremes is measured again at that depth (`+fit widening`) and put back into the station. The days' wider spread
   (6.1) is not held: a model with no cloud in its daily range cannot give it (DEBTS).
7. **The sun is checked against the satellite.** The Meinel beam on the ground and the diffuse sky under the weather's own
   cloud, integrated over a month's days, within an eighth of the Bureau's daily exposure for the site, every month: the
   check on the term that gets a naked founder through a winter day (FP.2) and on the cloud.
8. **The tables are kept and attributed.** `Data/stations/` holds the two pages' text as copied; `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`
   the Bureau's attribution; the constants' doc comments name the table and its years.
9. **The check reads more.** `eg2.weather` version 2 adds the wind, the cloud and the humidity to each sample (additive,
   the stride changed, the sidecar's fields authoritative); `weather_check.py` restates the Bureau's figures from the
   tables and holds the years to them with its own arithmetic: the air, the deciles, the rain and its days at the stated
   thresholds, the heavy days, the wind, the cloud and the humidity at nine and at three, and the sun on the ground under
   Meinel's beam restated.

## Non-goals

An inland gradient across the region from Nowra's record (the nights colder away from the sea, which the shore-distance
layer could carry): recorded here, owed in DEBTS. A daily series (the day-to-day autocorrelation of the fronts, the
dew point's spread). The wind by direction (the windroses were not copied). Drawing or hearing any of it (M1.8b).

## How it is proved

`dotnet test` over the whole tree, each new test's reason broken once and seen red; the Unity-shaped compile; the
almanac tool's `+fit all` printing the constants the engine carries and the model's months beside the record's;
`weather_check.py` on a year for each of 256 seeds at the lighthouse's height, every row green. The night the founder
lives (FP.2's `WarmthTests`) re-counted under the record's weather.
