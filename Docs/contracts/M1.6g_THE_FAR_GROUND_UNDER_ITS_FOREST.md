# Contract M1.6g — The far ground under its forest

**Status:** opened 2026-09-23, the day M1.6f thinned the far forest, and built and proved the same day (its record below);
the look waits on William's eyes (DEBTS, his table: "The far forest's look"). Owner: Claude (the main session).

## What was found before it was designed (2026-09-23)

M1.6f bounded the far forest's cost by drawing one far tree in 2^L at distance, each kept crown spread to cover in plan what
the dropped ones did. Its lookout frames (`Artefacts/frames/ff-lookout-20260923T060344Z`) show what that costs the eye:
seen from the side, a crown spread wide and not tall covers far less of the view than the trees it stands for, and between
the kept ones shows the ground beyond the held tiles, which is one flat tan everywhere (the region's coarse Terrain wears the
project's ground layer and nothing else). From the escarpment the forest a few kilometres off became flat slivers over bare
ground where it had been a speckle of clumps (the 22 September frames of the valley, `Artefacts/frames/valley-20260922`), and
over the 32 km valley the far hills read as a desert. The count drawn cannot go back up at 32 km (M1.6f's measure). What the
far forest needs to read as forest has to come from the ground under it.

## What this slice promises

1. **The ground beyond the held tiles wears its forest's colour.** `FarCanopy` (ClientCore) makes one picture of the whole
   region from the two far layers the client already holds for all of it (M1.6d): a texel a 40 m square, rows south to north,
   the ground's own colour where no tree stands and the square's trees' foliage over it by the share of the square their
   crowns cover (as many crowns as its count, as wide as its trees' height and their species' crown share make them). The
   region's coarse Terrain wears it in place of the flat ground layer once the far layers are held; the held tiles keep their
   own cover's colours, and the far skirt beyond the region its own layer.
2. **Nothing new on the wire and nothing on the frame.** Made once on a worker when the last far tile arrives; a texture of
   a texel a square (800 a side at 32 km, 200 at 8); the frame's cost unchanged.
3. **The look.** Lookout frames at both valleys and at Bherwerre, for William's eyes: the far forest green on its hills.

## Non-goals

No new layer, no change to the far trees' thinning. The cover of the ground beyond the held tiles stays unknown to the
client (it does not travel region-wide), so the ground between the trees is one colour: the ground's colour when no cover is
known.

## How it is proved

- Tests first: `FarCanopyTests` (a square full of tall trees is its species' foliage, a square of none is the bare ground, a
  square between by its crowns' share; each texel the square it stands for, south to north; a region with no far tiles held
  is bare).
- `dotnet test Engine/tests/EarthGame.Tests`; the Unity-shaped compile; the lookout frames at the valleys and at Bherwerre.
