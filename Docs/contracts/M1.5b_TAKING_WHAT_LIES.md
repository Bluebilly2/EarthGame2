# Contract M1.5b — Taking what lies

**Status:** opened 2026-09-11 on the owner's "continue work", after M1.5a; the second slice of
`M1.5_HANDS_AND_VERBS.md`; built the same day (the exit record, below). Owner: Claude (Opus 5). The owner's lane: the
frames, and his hands on the verb.

Since M1.6a the sticks and cobbles of a real world are counts in its loose layer, not entities, and M1.5a's verbs reach
only entities: a founder can pick up what `-eg-items` dropped and nothing the world itself laid down. This slice lets
them take the stick under a blackbutt and the cobble on a creek bed.

## What was found before it was designed

- The loose layer counts the sticks and the cobbles on each 4 m cell, to fifteen of each, and every client draws the
  k-th thing of a cell where `StandLayout` puts it. The world never changes the layer once made, and a client caches
  its tiles by checksum.
- The region file has carried a layer-diff count since M1.3 (`eg2.region` version 1) that has always been 0, and its
  reader refuses any other.
- The stone layer names the stone of every cell, but the server reads it from the world folder only at creation: a
  cobble taken up has to learn its stone then, or never.

## The decisions this rests on

**A taking names its thing, and the layer stays as made.** A thing lying is named by its cell's row and column, stick
or cobble, and its index — the name `StandLayout` already draws it by. What is taken is kept beside the layer as a bit
for each index (`LooseTaken`), never by lowering a count: a lowered count takes the last thing of the cell, not the
one the founder looked at. The layer's tiles, and every client's cached copy of them, stay as the world made them.

**Taken is taken.** A thing taken becomes an item under a new id and is put down as an entity, as every carried thing
is; nothing goes back into the layer.

**The client is told, not sent the tile again.** The server tells every client what has been taken, cell by cell: all
of it at the join, and each taking to every client as it happens — a few bytes, and a world has at most eight
founders. A client draws a cell less what was taken from it.

**A cobble is its stone.** A cobble taken up becomes an item of the stone the stone layer names on its cell
(`item/cobble-silcrete`, and one for every stone the engine knows), weighing by that stone's density; a cell with no
stone named gives the plain cobble.

## What this slice promises

1. **Naming.** `LyingThing` (row, column, kind, index) and a cell's centre by the raster's own rule, beside
   `StandLayout`, so the server's reach, the client's drawing and its crosshair find a thing in one way.
2. **The verb.** A pick-up names a lying thing (protocol v7: the Intent's second target kind), and the server commits
   it: the thing is there and not taken, within reach by M1.5a's rule, and the hands have room. It becomes a carried
   item under an id the entity store allocates and never uses again.
3. **The record.** `LooseTaken` is kept by the world, saved as the region files' layer diffs (`eg2.region` version 2;
   version 1 still read) and named in the digest. A save that says a thing was taken that its cell never held is
   refused. `save_check.py` reads the diffs, rebuilds their lines of the digest, and checks every taken thing against
   the loose layer it was taken from.
4. **Streaming.** `LooseTaken` messages (every cell at the join, each taking to every client); the client draws a
   cell less what was taken; the tile cache is untouched.
5. **The crosshair** offers a lying thing as it offers an entity ("a stick — pick up"), found by the same layout and
   the same mesh bounds the drawing uses.
6. **Stone cobbles.** An item definition for each stone, each drawn as the cobble is, and named by the carrying window.
7. **Tests and frames.** Engine and server tests of the naming, the taking and every refusal; a two-client test in
   which one founder's taking is seen by the other and survives a save and a reload with an identical digest, and a
   rejoin; frames at 1440p and 1080p, windowless and muted, of a stick in the litter looked at, taken (gone from the
   ground and in the hand), and put down.

`stand_check` and `tile_check` do not change: the layer files and the tiles a client caches are unchanged by a taking,
which lives in the region files that `save_check` reads.

## Non-goals

Trees felled (the stand layer's diffs); a thing put back into the layer; fibre and driftwood as things that lie; a
client's memory of takings far beyond its tiles, which is the map itself.

## How it is proved

world saved with things taken and carried; the frames at both resolutions; the owner's eyes. The gate `M1.5b`
(`Tools/gate/gates.json`) runs all but the last in order, the frames and the saved world by `Tools/world/carry.py
--scenario litter`.
