# Contract M1.6f — The far forest bounded

**Status:** opened 2026-09-23 as WG.2b's dependency D1 (`contracts/WG.2b_THE_WHOLE_VALLEY.md`): the whole valley is not put in
William's hands before it lands. Built and proved at 8 km and at 32 km the same day (its record below): the growth bounded,
the whole over its 2 ms target by the held tiles' own far band. Owner: Claude (the main session). The measure that opened it
is the side worker's.

## What was found before it was designed (2026-09-23)

The side worker's first-frame scenario, a held full turn at 1080p (WG.2b's trial table):

| World | Far trees placed | Median / p95 frame | With `-eg-hide far` |
|---|---|---|---|
| The valley, 8 km | 37,561 | 13.1 / 19.3 ms | 5.4 / 7.1 ms |
| A trial, 16 km | 149,299 | 15.5 / 28.2 ms | 5.5 / 7.4 ms |

The far forest (M1.6d) is 7.7 ms of the median at 8 km and 10 ms at 16, and it is the frame's only growth with the region. It
places one far tree for every 40 m square that holds any, over the whole region, and draws every one of them in the view at
every distance: the near tiles' own far band draws one tree in two past 500 m and one in four past a kilometre (M1.6a), and
the ring never thins at all. So the count drawn grows with the region's area: about 600,000 at WG.2b's 32 km.

## What this slice promises

1. **The ring thinned by distance, in nested levels.** Each far tree has a level from a hash of its own square
   (`FarTree.Level`, 0 to `FarForest.MostLevel`): a tree is kept at a level when its own level is at least that, so one in
   2^L is kept at level L, and a tree kept at a level is kept at every level below it; crossing a distance only drops or adds
   trees and never moves one. (Built with `MostLevel` 8, one in 256: see "What changed on the way".) Each tree kept at level L has its crown spread by √(2^L), so the canopy covers as much as it
   did. A block of the ring is drawn at the level its distance from the eye gives, one level more each time the distance
   grows by √2 past `FarForest.FullToM`, so the trees drawn stand as densely on the screen at every distance.
2. **The cost bounded.** The far forest at most 2 ms of the median frame at 16 km, WG.2b's target, measured as the side
   worker measured it (the first-frame scenario, a held turn at 1080p, with and without `-eg-hide far`): at 8 km on the
   valley now, and on WG.2b's whole valley when it exists.
3. **The look kept.** The lookout frames at the valley's vantages before and after, for William's eyes: a far forest as
   close and as dense to the eye as it was.

## Non-goals

No change to the far layers, the tiles or the protocol. No buffers kept on the GPU unless the bound by distance is not enough
(then this contract says so and grows a promise). The near tiles' far band keeps its own thinning.

## How it is proved

- Tests first: `FarForestTests` (the levels' shares within three standard deviations, nested, the same every time, the
  crowns' cover held by the spread), and `FarForest.LevelAt`'s distances.
- A sabotage: levels that do not halve (a level drawn as the hash mod five) turns the shares and the cover tests red; the
  nesting is the one level a tree carries, so no level can drop a tree a higher one keeps.
- `dotnet test Engine/tests/EarthGame.Tests` redirected to a file with its exit code; the Unity-shaped compile; the
  first-frame scenario at the valley with and without the far forest, before and after; the lookout frames.

## What it did (exit record, 2026-09-23, at 8 km)

**Built.** `FarTree.Level` from a hash of each square's own, mixed again from the one its place and yaw are drawn from;
`FarForest.LevelAt` (whole within 1,414 m, one level more each √2, to `MostLevel` 8, one in 256 past 16 km) and `SpreadAt`;
each block of the ring holds a list a level (`Block.FarLevels`, the trees of that level or higher, crowns spread by √(2^L))
and is drawn at the level its centre's distance gives, and a tile of the ring past 3 km (`RingWholeTileM`) in the view is
drawn whole at its centre's level, its blocks not each tested; `StandViews.RingDrawn` and the timing record's `far_drawn`
count what a frame drew.

**As run.** `dotnet test Engine/tests/EarthGame.Tests`: 836 passed, 0 failed (three new in `FarForestTests`: the levels'
shares 20,258, 10,022, 4,926 and 2,424 of 40,401 against 20,201, 10,100, 5,050 and 2,525, each within three standard
deviations; the spread crowns covering 1.003, 0.992, 0.975 and 0.960 of the whole forest's, each within three; the level's
distances). A sabotage, a level drawn as the hash mod five, turned both statistical tests red and was restored byte for byte.
The Unity-shaped compile clean.

**What changed on the way.**
1. *To one in 256.* The first cap, one in sixteen past 4 km, bounded the 8 km valley and not the 32 km one: most of the view
   there lies far past 4 km, where the count grew with the area again (14,669 drawn at the escarpment). Eight levels keep the
   trees drawn as dense on the screen out to 16 km, a crown spread sixteenfold there as wide to the eye as a whole one at a
   kilometre.
2. *Far tiles drawn whole.* At 32 km the ring holds 16,384 blocks; testing each against the view was a part of the frame that
   grew with the area. A tile past 3 km in the view is drawn whole at one level.
3. *The target.* The 2 ms was set by the side worker for the far forest as `-eg-hide far` measures it, which takes the held
   tiles' own far band with it. That band, 1.5 to 2.0 ms at 8 km and about 2 at 32, is the rest, the same at any size of
   region; thinning it harder changes the forest half a kilometre off, which is William's to see (DEBTS, his first table).
4. *The look, found in the lookout frames.* The build before M1.6f was replaced in Build/Harness before lookout frames of it
   were taken, and the 22 September frames of the valley (`Artefacts/frames/valley-20260922`) stand for it. Beside them the
   new far forest reads thinner: a crown spread wide and not tall covers in plan what the dropped trees did and far less of
   a view from the side, and between the kept ones shows the coarse Terrain's flat tan. Over the 32 km valley the far hills
   read as bare ground with slivers on them (`Artefacts/frames/ff-lookout-20260923T060344Z`). The count cannot go back up;
   the ground under the far forest is given its forest's colour instead (M1.6g).
