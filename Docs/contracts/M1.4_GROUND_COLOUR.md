# Contract M1.4d — The ground is the colour of what it is

> **Since:** the owner looked at the palette sheet, the map and the frames on 2026-09-10 and held off judging:
> "judging quality of the colour palette feels arbitrary when it is just a coloured smooth plane." It goes back
> to him in frames that have grain and things standing on the ground (DEBTS.md).

**Status:** opened 2026-09-10 after M1.4c closed. Owner: Claude (Opus 5). The owner's lane: the frames. A palette
is art direction and his eyes decide whether the country reads right. A wrong reading is either a debt against the
world's own layers or a change to the palette, never a reworded promise.

The frames of M1.4c show one tan everywhere, on a peninsula whose census says beach, dune, forest, heath and
swamp. The world knows what covers every four metres of itself — the plant community, the topology, the soil, the
wetness, the water — and none of it reaches the client. This slice sends what that comes to, and paints with it.

## What was measured before it was designed

Nine tiles around the wake, deflated exactly as the codec would (`Tools/` pricing on `Artefacts/worlds/gate`):

| what would travel | nine tiles |
|---|---|
| the ground itself, for scale | 417.9 KB |
| soil depth | 501.3 KB |
| wetness | 475.9 KB |
| understory | 135.8 KB |
| overstory | 124.5 KB |
| topology | 100.0 KB |
| stone | 13.4 KB |
| **one cover code a post** | **119.6 KB**, or **163.9 KB** with the wetness quarter |

Soil depth and wetness each cost more than the ground, because a continuous field over noisy country does not
compress. So the client is not sent the ingredients and asked to cook: it is sent what they come to.

## What this slice promises

1. **The world names the cover of every post.** `WorldLayers` computes one byte a cell from the layers it already
   holds — the water class, the topology, the plant community, the soil's depth and its wetness — and
   `WorldCreation` writes it as `cover.u8` with its legend, like every other layer. The low six bits are the
   cover; the top two are which quarter of the land's own wetness this cell falls in. The cascade is one function
   with one order, stated in its doc comment and restated in the layer's legend:

   sea, then fresh water, then wetland, then beach, then dune, then cliff or shore platform or soil too thin to
   hold anything, then what grows on it (shrub, bracken, herb, grass), then litter under a canopy with nothing
   growing under it, then bare earth. The understory is asked before the canopy because the ground under an open
   banksia is what you are standing on; the canopy is drawn as trees when trees are drawn.

2. **It travels as a layer.** `TileLayer.GroundCover = 3`, a layer of codes in tile format v2, cached under
   `ground-cover/`. Both ends already walk `TileLayers.All`, so a world that has no cover layer — every world
   saved before today — answers with a header of zero posts and the client draws as it did before.

3. **One palette, engine-free.** `GroundPalette` in `EarthGame.ClientCore` answers a code with a colour: each
   cover names its dry colour and its wet colour, and the wetness quarter sits between them. Nothing else in the
   client decides what the ground looks like, so a tuft of grass added later can ask the same question and get
   the same answer.

4. **The ground is drawn in it.** A tile's cover becomes a colour map at about a metre a texel — the cover posts
   blended between, so a beach does not end on a line — and that map is the terrain layer of that tile alone.
   The map is built in `EarthGame.ClientCore` as bytes and wrapped in a texture by the Unity layer, the same
   split as the water's quads. A tile whose cover has not arrived keeps the flat layer until it does.

5. **What is not coloured says so.** The coarse region ring under the tiles and the 64 km skirt keep the flat
   layer: their cover is a fact of a world, and the client reads the region's bake, not a world folder. The seam
   at the edge of the streamed tiles is stated here and recorded in DEBTS.md rather than implied.

6. **Tests.** The cascade against a made world (each branch reached, and the order proved where two could claim a
   cell); the packing (a cover and a quarter go in and come out); the palette (every cover answers, an unknown
   code answers with something rather than throwing, wet is darker than dry); the colour map (a tile of one cover
   is one colour, a boundary blends across it, a tile with no cover draws nothing); and the whole path from a
   created world through the wire to the map a view would build.

7. **A verifier, `cover_check.py`.** It recomputes the cover from the world's *other* layers in Python, by the
   cascade restated from this contract rather than from the engine's code, and compares it with the world's
   `cover.u8` and with the client's cached tiles. It prints both numbers and the region's shares by cover, which
   is the census's kind of statement: what this country is made of.

8. **Frames** at 1440p and 1080p, recorded windowless and muted from the built player: the wake, and the shore
   where sand meets what grows behind it, and Lake Windermere again so the water and the ground are judged
   together.

## Non-goals

Grain underfoot: a metre a texel is a colour, not a texture, and the ground will still be smooth at the boots.
The coarse ring (promise 5). Plants as objects; a canopy is not drawn, so a forest floor is lit as if the sky
were open. The sea's own colour and the water's, which have their material. The census gaining a cover table —
the census is the owner's document and it has a row he has not ruled on yet.

## What changed on the way

- **Every join was refused when the layer was added.** The protocol refused a tile layer above `WaterClass` by
  its number, so the highest layer lived in the enum and again in the reader. It is asked of the set now
  (`TileLayers.IsKnown`), and the same was true of which layers carry codes (`TileLayers.CarriesCodes`): the
  receiver had that as `== WaterClass`, so it took a cover tile as a run of int16 metres, failed the length
  check and dropped it without a word. Both are one fact with one owner now, and the streaming tests count the
  layers rather than the number three.
- **The first streamed run threw nine times.** `LoadRawTextureData` wants the whole mip chain; the one level goes
  in with `SetPixelData` and `Apply` makes the rest.
- **The map is two metres a texel, where promise 4 said about one.** Two texels a post is what a blend between
  4 m posts needs; a metre a texel is four times the work for the same raster, and it is the same colour either
  way. The promise keeps its words and this is the number that was built.
- **The cascade asked the canopy before the understory at first**, which made a third of the peninsula one
  colour. Corrected before any of it was drawn, by pricing it.
- **`cover_check`'s last row was set below its own arithmetic.** Three wetness cuts, each a storage step wide,
  already take 1.2 % of the posts; the bound is derived from the step now (2.45 %) instead of being a round
  number, and it still catches a wetness field that piled up on the cuts.
