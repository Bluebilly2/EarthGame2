# Contract M1.5a — The verb rule and carrying

**Status:** opened 2026-09-11 on the owner's "go", the first slice of `M1.5_HANDS_AND_VERBS.md`, and built the same
day (the exit record, below), after which the owner played it. Owner: Claude (Opus 5). The owner's lane: the controls
under his hands, and the frames.

CANON's verb rule (2026-08-26) is the brief: "what an action is comes from what you are looking at and what is in
your hand… Hold left mouse = work on it; right mouse = use it; Tab = carrying; wheel or 1–9 = what is in hand." This
slice makes that rule real for the first verbs a founder has — pick up, put down, choose what is in hand — for the
items the world already holds as entities. The generated sticks and cobbles follow in M1.5b.

## The decisions this rests on

**A carried thing is out of the world.** Picking a thing up takes it out of the entity store and into the carrier's
record; putting it down returns it, with the id it always had, where the founder puts it, and it falls. The world
then holds only what lies in it: nothing follows a founder about, nothing carried is anyone else's to be shown, and
the carrier's record, saved in the player file, is where what they carry lives while they are away. (The other way,
a carried entity that moves with its carrier and is hidden from everyone else, touches the interest set, the region
files and every walk over the store for no gain yet.)

**Nine places and a hand.** A founder carries up to nine things, one to each of the keys 1–9; one place is the hand,
chosen by those keys or the wheel. Picking up fills the first free place and, when the hand is empty, the hand.
Limits by weight wait for the body to feel weight (M2).

**The server commits every verb.** The client asks, naming the verb and its target; the server checks that the
target is there and within reach, 4 m from the founder's eye (v1's reach), and answers with what happened. The eye's
height, 1.65 m standing and 1.1 m crouching, moves from the client's camera into the engine, so the server's reach
and the camera are one number.

## What this slice promises

1. **The rule's controls.** `InputSystem_Actions.inputactions`, Unity's template until now, is rewritten to the
   rule: move, look, jump, sprint, crouch, work (hold left mouse), use (right mouse), carrying (Tab), the wheel and
   1–9 for the hand, and Escape; it is the one owner of every binding: the code names actions and binds no key of its
   own (an action the asset lacks does nothing and is named in the log), the build refuses an asset that lacks one,
   and a source rule refuses a key in code. The scripted seam gains the new actions.
2. **Intents on the wire (protocol v6).** `Intent` (client to server: a sequence, a verb, its target — an entity
   to pick up, a point to put down at, a place to hold), `IntentResult` (done, or why not: out of reach, not there,
   hands full, nothing in hand, not now) and `Carrying` (to the carrier: what is in each place and which is the hand,
   at the join and on every change). An entity taken up is gone to its other viewers with a reason of its own.
3. **Carrying, saved and named.** The player file moves to version 3 with the hand and what is carried (the thing's
   id, key, spawn tick and place); version 2 is still read. The world's digest names what each founder carries, and
   `save_check.py` rebuilds those lines by hand.
4. **Pick up, put down, hold.** Committed by the server as above; a thing put down returns with its own id, where the
   founder put it, and falls to the ground the server holds.
5. **The crosshair says the verb.** What is looked at within reach is named on the HUD's verb line with what the
   right mouse would do to it ("a stick — pick up"; with something in hand and the ground under the crosshair, "put
   down the stick"); an answer other than done is said there too.
6. **The Tab window.** A read-only overlay of the nine places: what is in each, its mass, and which is the hand. The
   world runs on while it is open (v1's I1 contract).
7. **One look for a stick.** The thing in hand is drawn in front of the camera, and an item lying in the world is
   drawn, from the stand's own meshes (`StandMeshes`, from `StandForms`): M1.3's prefab registry and its placeholder
   prefabs go, paying DEBTS' "a stick lying and a stick dropped are drawn by two owners", and the crosshair picks a
   thing by its mesh's bounds.
8. **A falling thing is drawn between its states,** as a remote body is, paying DEBTS' "a falling thing is drawn in
   twenty steps a second".
9. **Tests and frames.** Engine and server tests of every verb's acceptance and refusal; a two-client test in which one
   founder's pick-up and put-down are seen by the other; a save and a reload with an identical digest while things are
   carried; a founder who leaves and joins again still carrying what they carried. Frames at 1440p and 1080p,
   windowless and muted: a stick picked up from `-eg-items`, in hand, the Tab window open, the stick put down again.

Amended 2026-09-11 while building, before the exit: promise 1 said the code's fallback would read the asset, and
promise 7 that the prefabs would keep their colliders. A fallback has nothing to read once the asset is gone, so the
build refuses such an asset instead; and the prefabs had no colliders, so they go whole.

## Non-goals

The generated sticks and cobbles (M1.5b); work verbs — knapping, cutting, stripping (M2); throwing; weight limits;
other founders' hands; death, which does not exist yet (Standard death will lay what was carried where the founder
fell, which this design allows); rebinding in a settings page (M1.9).
