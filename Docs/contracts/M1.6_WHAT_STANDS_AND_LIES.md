# Contract M1.6a — What stands and lies on the ground

**Status:** opened 2026-09-10 on the owner's "go" for three jobs in order, of which this is the third: build the
generator for what stands and lies on the ground. Landed 2026-09-11; the exit record closes this file. Owner: Claude (Opus 5). The owner's lane: the frames, and with
them the ground's colour, which he deferred in M1.4d until things stand on it.

CANON ruling 22 is the brief: the world starts in a generated state, from the facts it holds — "a stone lies where
that stone crops out, a stick where that tree stands" — and changes from there; the history that put a thing where
it is is not simulated. M1.2 grew the plants as layers, one species to a 4 m cell. Nothing stands on the ground
yet and nothing lies on it: the owner, playing, has never seen a thing in the world, because M1.3's items appear
only when a run passes `-eg-items`.

## What was found before it was designed

**The architecture's line does not survive the numbers.** ARCHITECTURE §5 says trees inside the interest radius are
entities. The radius is 1,500 m (`ServerConfig.InterestRadiusM`), about 7 km² round each player, which at the stand
densities below is of the order of 70,000 trees a player. Every entity inside the radius goes out in the join
snapshot with no byte budget, `EntityStore.Within` walks every entity for every session every tick, and the client
makes a GameObject for each (the code survey of 2026-09-10). The same trees as a layer of codes cost a few kilobytes
a tile.

**Nothing materialises.** No code creates or retires entities by where the players are; the slow scheduler has no
real layer; and when a world is opened the server reads only its ground, its water and its cover, not its plants
or its stone.

**The tile format already carries what this needs.** Tile format v2 carries layers of codes, a byte a post at the
raster's own 4 m pitch through deflate (M1.4b, M1.4d); a layer that is mostly zero costs almost nothing.

**What the place says.** Bangalay Sand Forest, the community of the peninsula's sand plain, has a "dense to open
tree canopy, approximately 5 - 20 m tall, depending on exposure and disturbance history", bangalay and coast banksia
its commonest trees, an open shrub stratum of old-man banksia and coast teatree, and a ground layer of flax-lilies,
*Lepidosperma* and *Lomandra longifolia* (NSW BioNet, the threatened ecological community's profile, read
2026-09-10). Fallen wood on Australian forest floors runs from about 19 t/ha in woodland to 134 t/ha in tall open
forest (Woldendorp and Keenan 2005, *Austral Ecology* 30: 834–843, as their review reports it). A benchmark of stems
a hectare by diameter class for south-eastern Australian forests exists (Gibbons and others 2010, *Forest Ecology
and Management* 260: 2125–2133), but its tables could not be read here; it is a sourcing debt, not a number this
contract leans on.

**What v1 built.** v1's `EucalyptBuilder` grew trees as faceted geometry rather than primitives: a crooked five-sided
trunk, one or two bare limbs, three to six clumps of leaves with sky between them; twelve variants built once and
scaled per tree, coloured in the vertices under one material, deterministic from an integer hash. Its forms were the
Southern Highlands'. The plan names it as the tree's skeleton (plan §3, flora).

## The decision this rests on

**What stands and lies is a layer until something moves it.** The world computes, when it is created, where each
tree stands and what lies on each 4 m cell, and saves both as layers like every other; they travel as code tiles
and the client draws them. A thing becomes an entity only when something moves it — a verb picking up a stick, a
tree felled — which is M1.5's and later, and the region file's reserved count of layer diffs is where a world will
record that a generated thing is gone. ARCHITECTURE §5's line is amended, with the numbers above, in the commit
that lands this.

## What this slice promises

1. **The world stands its trees.** `WorldLayers` places individual trees from the canopy layer it already holds.
   Where a canopy stands, trunks are placed so that their crowns cover the ground about as much as the canopy
   layer says it is covered: a crown's width follows the tree's height by its species' form, no two trunks stand
   nearer than their crowns allow, and which cell holds a trunk is the seed's. Each tree carries the canopy's species
   at its cell and the height that species grows to there (`PlantSpecies.HeightAt`). Written as `stand.u8`, a byte a
   cell: zero where no trunk stands, else the species and a height class, both stated in the layer's legend.
2. **What lies is where it would be.** `loose.u8`, a byte a cell: sticks fallen under the trees that stand, within
   a crown and more of them under the bigger trees and the species that shed more; and cobbles where the stone
   layer and the ground say stone lies loose — beaches, shore platforms, creek beds, and thin soil over the stone of
   the older surfaces — and none on a dune or deep soil. The counts follow from what stands and what the ground is,
   never from a spawn table.
3. **Where exactly, one owner.** Within its cell a trunk's offset and yaw, and every stick's and cobble's, come from
   an integer hash of the cell (`StandLayout`, in the engine), so the server and the client put each thing at the
   same centimetre from whole numbers alone, and a later verb can name the second stick of a cell.
4. **They travel as layers.** `TileLayer.Stand = 4` and `TileLayer.Loose = 5`, layers of codes in tile format v2.
   The server reads both when a world is opened and serves them as it serves the cover; both ends walk
   `TileLayers.All`, and a world made before today answers with headers of zero posts, as the cover's did.
5. **They are drawn.** The client draws the trees from v1's `EucalyptBuilder`, ported to the URP client with the
   forms of Bherwerre's five tall plants (blackbutt, bangalay, old-man banksia, coast banksia, swamp paperbark), a
   handful of variants each, instanced and scaled by height, with a near form and a cheap far form; and sticks and
   cobbles, instanced, within a stated distance. One owner: a table in ClientCore says what each species looks
   like, and nothing else does.
6. **Budgets measured.** The frame's cost of the trees and of the loose things against the plan's 2.0 ms and 0.7 ms,
   by the plan's measurement protocol; the bytes a join spends on the two layers and the time to interactive against
   N1's 10 s; the counts inside the 3 × 3 tiles round a player. A cost over its budget is a debt with its number.
7. **Tests** against a made world where the answer is known: trunks only where a canopy stands, of the canopy's
   species, their crowns covering about what the layer covers and never nearer than their crowns allow; sticks
   only under trees, cobbles only where stone lies loose; the hash putting the same thing in the same place on every
   call; the codes packed and unpacked; and the whole path from a created world through the wire to what a view
   would draw.
8. **A verifier, `stand_check.py`.** It restates this contract's rules in numpy against the world's other layers and
   the client's cached tiles: every trunk on a canopied cell of its own species; the crowns' cover against the
   canopy layer's; heights inside each species' range, and the sand forest's trees inside its 5–20 m; stems a
   hectare by community, printed beside the design's range; sticks only within crowns; cobbles only where stone lies
   loose. Both numbers on every row. `tile_check` gains the two layers.
9. **Frames** at 1440p and 1080p, recorded windowless and muted from the built player: M1.4d's three vantages and the
   new wake, with trees standing and things lying on the ground.

## Non-goals

Picking anything up, felling, stripping bark (M1.5's verbs and after); a layer diff's format beyond the count the
region file already reserves; trees as entities; shrubs, grass and litter as instances (the rest of M1.6); a trunk
the founder cannot walk through (collision on both ends; DEBTS.md); wind in the crowns; forest on the far skirt; bark
and leaf textures, where v1's vertex colour serves.

## How it is proved

- `dotnet test Engine/EarthGame.slnx` and the Unity-layer compile: exit 0, the count as run.
- `python Tools/verifiers/checks/stand_check.py` and `tile_check.py`: exit 0, both numbers on every row.
- A gate `M1.6`: the suite, the Unity-layer compile, the gate world created, `stand_check`, one shaped join from a
  built player and `tile_check` on what it cached. Gates `M1.2` and `M1.4-streaming` stay green.
- The join over the M1.B-shaped socket (`Tools/world/stream.py`): bytes and time to interactive, before and after.
- The frame's cost measured as the plan's protocol states it.
- Frames at both resolutions, sent to the owner.

## Exit

## Exit record (2026-09-11)

Landed on 2026-09-11, with the frames in the owner's hands. Promise by promise, what was run:

1. **The world stands its trees.** The Bherwerre world of seed 1347 stands 992,519 trees. `stand_check` on the gate
   world: every trunk under a canopy of its own species, no two nearer than their crowns allow, 0.739 of the
   1,543,096 canopied cells under a crown, every height inside its species' range.
2. **What lies is where it would be.** 3,348,011 sticks in 1,405,905 cells, none beyond a crown and a cell of a
   trunk; 209,024 cobbles in 89,064 cells, none on a dune, under water, or on deep soil that is no platform, cliff,
   beach or bed.
3. **Where exactly, one owner.** `StandLayout`; `StandPreparationTests` put a tree drawn from a streamed tile at the
   centimetre the layout names.
4. **They travel as layers.** Tile layers 4 and 5; `StreamingTests.WhatStandsAndLiesArrivesPostForPost`; on the
   gate's shaped join `tile_check` finds 0 posts of the 9 stand tiles and 0 of the 9 loose tiles that disagree with
   the world.
5. **They are drawn.** Frames at the four vantages in 1440p and 1080p, windowless and muted (`Artefacts/frames/m16a-3`,
   and beside M1.2b's in its `vantages_before_after.png`). Three faults were found on the way and fixed. The build
   stripped the shader's instanced variants, so the first player held its trees and none stood in its frames
   (`Artefacts/frames/m16a`); a material asset now asks for them. The first trees held lone clumps up on hair-thin
   limbs (`m16a-2`); limbs are thicker, and the clumps sit along the outer limb, two at least to each. A player with
   no graphics device threw on every frame it drew, 1,638 times in the first shaped join; it now makes no view, and
   the join logs 0 errors.
6. **Budgets.** Over, and a debt with its numbers. The trees took 10.5 ms of the median frame at the wake and 15.2 ms
   beside Windermere, against 2.0 ms; the sticks and cobbles 1.8 ms and 0.5 ms, against 0.7 ms. Measured without a
   window (the recorder's `-eg-hold`: a full turn, the CPU's work and the GPU's end to end), which bounds a pipelined
   frame from above; §8's visible-window protocol is still owed. The join: interactive 1.29 s after connecting (N1
   allows 10 s; 1.43 s on 2026-09-10, before this slice), on the ground as before; the two layers follow it, 110,056
   bytes of stand and 119,421 of loose for the nine tiles, and the join's 30 s walk received 689,267 bytes where it
   received 458,725 before. The nine tiles round the wake hold 110,063 trees, 379,456 sticks and 44,475 cobbles;
   round Windermere 176,906, 620,545 and 21,495; round the new wake 103,850, 363,665 and 38,832.
7. **Tests.** 322 pass, and the Unity-layer compile is clean.
8. **`stand_check`**, eight rows with both numbers on each, is green on the gate world, and `tile_check` holds the
   two layers post for post. Gates M1.6, M1.2 and M1.4-streaming are green.
9. **Frames** sent to the owner on 2026-09-11.

What changed on the way: bangalay's ceiling came down from 25 m to 20 m, the sand forest's, because the first world
stood 1,260 of its 105,821 sand-forest trees above BioNet's 20 m (up to 23.75 m); and `stand_check` reads the
thin-soil line to half the soil layer's 1 cm step, because 66 cobbled cells sat where the engine found the soil just
under 0.25 m and the layer wrote it as 0.25 m.

Debts left (DEBTS.md): a founder walks through every trunk; the stem-density benchmark is unread; a stick lying and a
stick dropped are drawn by two owners; no tree stands beyond the nine tiles; the trees cost five to eight times their
share of the frame.

The owner decides whether what stands and lies on the ground reads as this country, and the ground's colour he
deferred in M1.4d until things stood on it.

### The trees' cost, worked the same day (2026-09-11, the owner's "continue")

The debt of promise 6 was worked on after the landing. What a frame draws is now chosen by 64 m block (far blocks
only in the view, near blocks out of the view into the shadows alone, near trees casting only where the sun lets
their shadows reach the shadow distance); far trees thin with distance and share their corners; and no instance
takes a probe lookup or an inverse matrix. Measured the same way, on a freshly created world in the morning: the
trees cost 4.1 ms of the median frame beside Windermere and 2.9 ms at the wake (from 15.2 ms and 10.5 ms), each band
alone 2.1 ms near and 2.4 ms far beside Windermere; the sticks and cobbles 0.4 ms and 0.3 ms, inside their 0.7 ms.
The frames are `Artefacts/frames/m16a-7`, beside M1.2b's in its `vantages_before_after.png`. The trees are still
twice their share: the debt stays, with what is left named in it.
