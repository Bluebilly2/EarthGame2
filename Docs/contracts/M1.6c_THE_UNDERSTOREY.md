# Contract M1.6c — The understorey

**Status:** opened 2026-09-12 on the owner's "go", after M1.6b, as the M1.6 draft's second slice
(`M1.6_THE_REST_OF_THE_FLORA.md`). Owner: Claude (Opus 5). The owner's lane: whether the ground reads as this
country's heath, sedge, bracken and grass, and the frames.

## What was found before it was designed (2026-09-12)

- **The client already knows what grows underfoot.** Every 4 m post of the cover tile (M1.4d) carries a
  `GroundCover` in its low six bits — heath, bracken, sedge, grass, forest floor, swamp floor, sand, rock, bare
  earth, water — and the quarter of the land's own wetness in its top two, so a dry spur and a wet gully of the same
  cover are told apart. No new layer is needed, and none is added here.
- **What the cover cannot say** is which plant of a shape: a grass tree and a heath banksia are both heath, lomandra
  and saw-sedge both sedge. The drawn understorey is therefore of shapes, not species, and this contract says so
  rather than pretending otherwise.
- **The stand's machinery fits.** `StandLayout` places a thing inside its cell from a hash of the cell, so the same
  tuft stands in the same centimetre in every client and every run; `StandPreparation` places the trees and the
  litter that way, `StandViews` draws them instanced in one material, and `-eg-hide` parts what each costs.
- **The budget.** ARCHITECTURE §8 gives grass and the understorey 3.0 ms of the frame. The trees are at 3.0 to 4.4 ms
  of their 2.0 (DEBTS), so the understorey has no room to be generous with.
- **v1 drew it** as tufts baked into one mesh a 16 m cell within 70 m of the founder — a tussock, a lomandra clump, a
  bracken frond, a low rosette — denser where the soil was deeper and taller where it was wetter (`GrassField`). It
  baked because its instanced draw drew nothing; M1.6a's material asset has made instanced drawing work here.

## What this slice promises

1. **The ground grows what the cover says.** Heath stands as low woody shrubs, sedge as strap-leaved clumps, bracken
   as fronds and grass as tussocks, placed by the cell's own hash so every client draws the same tuft in the same
   place; a swamp floor grows clumps, a forest floor grows nothing (its litter is already drawn), and sand, rock,
   bare earth and water grow nothing. How many and how tall follow the cover and its wetness quarter.
2. **Near the founder only,** out to a stated distance the budget decides, from the tiles the client already holds,
   with nothing new on the wire.
3. **Not through a trunk.** A tuft whose cell carries a tree keeps out of its bark.
4. **Drawn instanced** in the stand's own material, with `-eg-hide understorey` to part what it costs from the rest,
   and the cost stated against §8's 3.0 ms, measured as M1.6a's was: the recorder's `-eg-hold` on freshly created
   worlds, the faster of two passes.
5. **A census probe.** A run records what the drawn understorey holds round a vantage — how many tufts of each shape
   within a stated radius — beside what the cover layer says grows there, so that what is drawn can be held against
   the world rather than admired.
6. **Frames** at M1.6a's four vantages, 1440p and 1080p, windowless and muted, for the owner's eyes.
7. **Tests.** The tufts of a cell are the cover's shape, in the cell, the same every time; a wetter quarter grows
   more and taller; water, rock, sand and a forest floor grow none; a tuft stands on the tile's ground and out of a
   trunk's bark.

## Non-goals

Which plant of a shape (a tile of understorey codes is priced in the M1.6 draft, not built here); the understorey as
anything a founder can pick, push through or be slowed by (M2's fibre and cordage); wind; the far ground beyond the
tiles the client holds (M1.6d); fauna.

## How it is proved

`dotnet test` and the Unity-layer compile, the count as run; `python Tools/unity/editmode.py`; the frame's cost by
`-eg-hold` at the four vantages on freshly created worlds, with and without `-eg-hide understorey`; the census probe's
two counts beside each other; the frames; the owner's eyes.

## What it did (exit record, 2026-09-13)

1. **The ground grows what the cover says.** `Understorey` reads the cover byte the server already streams (M1.4d):
   heath stands as low shrubs, sedge as strap-leaved clumps, bracken as fronds, grass as tussocks and a swamp floor as
   clumps, while sand, dune sand, rock, bare earth, water and a forest floor grow nothing. How many stand on a square
   metre (0.19 for heath and a swamp floor, 0.25 sedge, 0.375 bracken, 0.5 grass) and how tall they stand (0.45 to
   0.9 m) are the cover's, by the square metre and not by the cell so that a world of another raster grows the same
   ground; the wetness quarter beside the cover grows them two-thirds thicker and a third taller from the driest
   quarter to the wettest. Every tuft is placed inside its cell by `StandLayout`'s hash, as the trees and the litter
   are, so the same tuft stands in the same centimetre in every client and every run, and each is its own size, yaw
   and one of six shapes of its kind.
2. **Near the founder only:** out to 45 m, placed again when they have walked 6 m or when a cover tile arrives, and
   placed to 51 m so that a walk never reaches the edge of what was placed. Nothing new travels for it.
3. **Not through a trunk:** a cell that carries a tree keeps its tufts out of the bark (`StandForms.TrunkRadiusAt` at
   the ground, the same radius M1.6b stops a founder at).
4. **Drawn instanced** in the stand's own material, one list for each of the four shapes and six variants, casting no
   shadows, with the shader drawing only what stands within the 45 m; `-eg-hide understorey` draws none, which is how
   the cost below was parted from the rest.
5. **The census probe** rides in every first-frame run's end record: how many tufts of each shape the client placed
   round the founder, beside how many cells of each cover the layer puts within the same 45 m.
6. **The shapes** are `StandMeshes.Tuft`: blades for a tussock, longer arching straps for a clump, a stalk and a leaf
   for each frond, and two or three little crowns on woody stems for a heath shrub — every blade and leaf drawn on
   both sides, since a leaf has no thickness. Their colours are `StandForms.TuftLow`/`TuftHigh`.
7. **Tests.** The cover's own shape on each cell, in the cell, the same every time; a wetter quarter thicker and
   taller; sand, rock, water and a forest floor bare; a tuft on the tile's ground and out of a trunk's bark; a cell
   carrying at most so many however thick the cover.

*The cost*, measured as M1.6a's rounds were — the recorder's `-eg-hold 20`, a full turn with every frame rendered and
waited for, on freshly created worlds in the morning, each configuration the faster of two passes, the two passes run
in opposite orders so that the world's own clock cannot favour one: at the new wake the median frame is 6.7 ms with
the understorey and 6.3 ms without it, and beside Windermere 6.8 ms against 6.7 ms. **The understorey costs 0.1 to
0.4 ms of the median frame, against §8's 3.0 ms.** Placing it costs the main thread 0.26 to 0.52 ms, once every six
metres walked.

**What changed on the way.** The first four vantage runs drew no understorey in their first frame: the tufts were
placed once, before the cover tiles had arrived, and not placed again until the founder had walked six metres, which
the wake frame is taken before. They are now placed again whenever the number of cover tiles held changes, and the
first frame of every vantage shows what grows there.

**Owed.** The owner's eyes: whether the ground reads as this country's heath, sedge, bracken and grass, and whether
the tufts are the right size and thickness. Every number of them but the covers' own names is chosen, and they are
the easiest thing in this slice to change.
