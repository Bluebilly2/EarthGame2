## What this slice promises

1. **Layer streaming on join (protocol v3).** The client stops reading the region raster from disk. On Welcome it
   asks the server for the nine 1 km tiles around its spawn on the region's fixed tile grid; the server answers
   with `TileHeader` and `TileChunk` messages (int16 centimetres, row-delta, deflate; a checksum in the header);
   the client assembles, verifies, caches to disk by checksum, and builds its collidable terrain from them. A tile
   whose checksum the client already holds is not sent again. The coarse region and the 64 km skirt stay static
   client data read from disk, stated as such: they are the bake, not the world, until a layer diverges from it.
2. **Interest and the mirror.** The server sends a player's state only to sessions within the interest radius;
   the client keeps a `RemoteMirror` per remote player and interpolates it with a stated delay. The server logs
   every accepted body per tick so the mirror's error against the authority is measurable, not estimated.
3. **Rejoin.** A session that drops, with or without a leave, keeps its body on the server for the life of the
   server (and in the world folder on save); a Hello with the same name wakes where it was, and supersedes the
   old session if the transport has not yet noticed the cut. The server exposes a world digest (clock, tick, bodies; whole
   micrometres and nanohours, so no runtime's printing of a double enters) on its console and logs a bodies digest per tick; the client logs the digest of its
   mirror after a rejoin, so N3's rows are recomputable.
4. **Measurement in the server.** Step durations per tick (the host supplies the clock; the engine-free server
   reads none), bytes per connection, managed heap, and the host's working set, written to the server's run log
   (`eg2.run` v1, new kinds documented in ARCHITECTURE §10).
5. **The send cap** as a harness condition: `UdpOptions.SendCapBytesPerSecond`, a token bucket per connection.
6. **Scenarios in the client:** `walk` (ten minutes of legal input on a route around the wake that crosses the
   water's edge, a rock platform and a dune crest, corrections logged with their displacement and segment),
   `soak` (thirty minutes of loops with one-second samples of the mirror and the remote count), `rejoin` (a walk
   cut and resumed without a clean leave), and `join` (connect, stream, log `interactive`). A scenario drives the
   founder through the scripted input seam; the recorder's `first-frame` remains.
7. **The harness** `Tools/corpus/run.py` launches the dedicated server with the simulator and the cap, and two
   headless players with scenarios; drives the console for the held-tick rejoin; collects every run log under
   `Artefacts/corpus/<stamp>/`; exits 0 only when every scenario answered, matched by mtime.
8. **`join_check.py`** recomputes N1–N4 from the run logs alone, independent of the harness's own summary, and
   is the M1.B gate's verifier.
9. **The numbers, as run**, pasted below, and the decision stated: pass; or one further contracted week; or
   plan B (FishNet, at its true cost: `EarthGame.Server`'s replication and its dotnet tests, and the pure-.NET
   dedicated server).

## The criteria (restated from ARCHITECTURE §7.1; that text governs)

- **N1** time-to-interactive ≤ 10 s at 100 ms RTT ± 20 ms, 2 % loss, 10 Mbit/s cap; ≤ 20 s at 200 ms, 5 %,
  5 Mbit/s. Interactive: nine tiles held, collidable ground under the player, first entity snapshot applied.
- **N2** ≤ 0.5 corrections per player-minute over the ten-minute walk; ≤ 2 per minute on the divergence segment;
  no correction displacing more than 1.0 m; the same in SOLO and at 100 ms / 2 %.
- **N3** held-tick: digest before the cut equals digest after the rejoin (except the session record); the
  rejoined mirror equals the server's digest of the interest subset; rejoin interactive ≤ 3 s at 100 ms / 2 %
  with cached tiles; heap growth ≤ 5 MB over ten cycles. Live-tick: the same without the before/after row, the
  mirror matching the server's current digest.
- **N4** thirty minutes, two clients, 100 ms / 2 %: zero crashes or uninduced disconnects; working set ≤ 1 GB;
  heap at minute 30 ≤ 1.2 × heap at minute 5; mirror within 0.5 m in ≥ 99 % of one-second samples and 2.0 m in
  all; remote counts equal in ≥ 99 % of samples and never diverging > 2 s; ≥ 99.9 % of ticks inside 50 ms with
  p95 ≤ 5 ms; ≤ 20 KB/s per client outside the join stream.

## Error budget and non-goals

No entities but players (the "entity snapshot" is the set of player states); no held items yet (M1.3 brings
the entity store, and dirty masks with it); no prediction switch; no encryption; no NAT traversal. The walk's
cliff-edge segment is whatever cliff the route can reach in ten minutes from the wake; Steamers Head is M1.5's.

## How it is proved

- `dotnet test Engine/EarthGame.slnx`: exit 0, count as run (tile codec round trips; interest filtering; mirror
  interpolation; rejoin keeps the body; digests are stable; tick stats; the send cap).
- `python Tools/corpus/run.py --scenarios join,walk,rejoin,soak`: exit 0, every scenario answered (`--quick` is
  a smoke run and never the numbers; `--player` names a build other than `Build/Player/EarthGame2.exe`).
- `python Tools/verifiers/checks/join_check.py Artefacts/corpus/<stamp>`: every row printed with its number and
  its threshold; exit 0. The gate reads `Artefacts/corpus/latest`, which the harness points at its last run.
- `python Tools/gate/run_gate.py M1.B`: green.

## Exit

The closing commit carries the four criteria's numbers as run and the decision. If a criterion fails, the one
further week is contracted in writing before any more code is written.

## The decision, as the numbers read

correction in thirty player-minutes of legal walking including the bank and the shore, the held-tick digest equal
across ten cuts, the mirror within half a metre in every sample of the soak, no server update over the tick
interval in thirty-six thousand, and a fifteenth of the bandwidth budget. Claude's reading is **pass**: the
hand-rolled netcode over the engine-free core stands, and plan B (FishNet, with the loss of the pure-.NET server and
its dotnet tests) is not taken. The ruling is the owner's and is entered in CANON.md when he makes it; nothing in
this slice pre-empts it.
