Drawing it is the half after.

## What this slice promises

1. **The tile format's second version.** A tile names the layer it carries. A metres layer packs as version 1 did
   (each post as centimetres in a signed 16-bit integer, each row its first value then the difference to the post
   before, the whole run through deflate); a code layer packs its raw codes through deflate, which is what an id
   layer with long runs of one value wants. `TileCodec.Version` becomes 2 and ARCHITECTURE §10's row moves with it.
2. **Water travels as depth, not as a second ground.** The layer a client needs for water is the world's
   `surface` (the water's surface where water stands, the ground elsewhere), and sending it whole costs almost
   what the ground costs: measured on the Bherwerre world, nine tiles are 277 KB as a metres layer of its own
   against 300 KB for the heights. The client already holds those heights, so what it does not know is only the
   difference: **the same nine tiles are 31 KB as depth over the tile's own heights**, since the depth is exactly
   zero over land and deflate is left with the 23 % of the region that is under water. A depth tile is refused for
   a tile whose heights the client does not hold, and the surface it reconstructs is exact to the centimetre
   because both are quantised the same way.
3. **The wire's fifth version.** `TileWant`, `TileHeader` and `TileChunk` carry the layer as a byte, wire-visible
   and never renumbered: 0 the ground, 1 the water's depth over it, 2 the water's class. `ProtocolInfo.Version`
   becomes 5 and a version-4 Hello is refused with both numbers.
4. **The server serves what the world holds.** `WorldPreparation` loads the world folder's `surface` and `water`
   beside its heights and `WorldState` holds them; a world without them (a save older than M1.2) serves the
   ground alone and says so in one line rather than pretending. `TileService` encodes a tile of a layer when it is
   first asked for and keeps the bytes; `BytesServed` counts every layer.
5. **The client holds them, and the join is not made to wait.** `TileReceiver` and `DiskTileCache` are keyed by
   layer as well as tile, so a cached ground tile is still a cached ground tile and the two new layers arrive
   beside it. The client asks for all three layers of the nine tiles around its spawn. **Interactive keeps the
   meaning CANON ruling 11 gave it** — the nine ground tiles answered, the snapshot applied, the tile under the
   founder built — and the water layers do not gate it: N1 measures the same thing after this slice as before.
6. **Residency, with a number.** A client holds at most 25 tiles of each layer. The tile under the founder and its
   eight neighbours are never evicted; beyond them the tile whose centre is farthest from the founder goes first.
   A test walks a client the width of the region and asserts the bound is never exceeded, the tile underfoot is
   never dropped, and a tile dropped and re-entered is answered from the disk cache rather than the wire.
7. **The budget, measured.** On the built player at M1.B's shaping (100 ms ± 20 ms, 2 % loss, 10 Mbit/s), a join
   records the bytes received to interactive and the bytes for all three layers, both stated as run. The join
   stays inside N1's ten seconds, and the increase over the ground alone is stated as a fraction rather than
   described.
8. **A verifier, `tile_check.py`.** It reads the client's disk cache with Python and zlib alone, unpacks each
   cached tile by the format §10 states, and compares it with the world folder's own layer, cell for cell: the
   ground to the centimetre, the depth by the surface it reconstructs, the class exactly. It prints how many
   cells it compared and the largest disagreement beside the tolerance, and it names the world folder and the
   cache it read. It never imports `TileCodec`.

## What changed on the way

- **A defect the measurement found, fixed first.** Pricing the water meant subtracting the ground from the
  surface, which showed 3,500 cells — 5.6 ha — whose lake surface stood below its own bed, by up to 0.486 m, all
  in lakes read off the ground rather than from a mapped outline. A lake now covers only the ground beneath its
  own surface, which is the rule a mapped outline already followed (its own commit, and DEBTS is unchanged
  because nothing is owed).
- **Promise 6's number was exercised at nine, not twenty-five.** The bound is 25 tiles a layer; the residency
  test sets it to 9 on a four-kilometre region of sixteen tiles, because a test that never reaches its bound
  proves nothing. The gate's real join holds nine of each, which is the founder's own tile and its neighbours.
- **The join asks for a layer it may not get.** A world saved before M1.2 has no water; the client learns that
  from one refused header per layer and stops asking. `StreamingTests` states both numbers.
- **A tolerance sat exactly on its bound.** The ground's worst disagreement is 0.005000000000002558 m: half a
  centimetre, which is what rounding to int16 centimetres costs, and two femtometres of floating point. The
  verifier's bound carries a nanometre of slack and says why, in the file and in its printed row.

What M1.4 still owes, unchanged from the contract's non-goals: drawing the lakes, the sea and the creeks from
what the client now holds; wading on it, which changes what the server accepts and so waits for the owner's M1.B
ruling; the layers this slice does not carry; and the ground-colour oracle.
