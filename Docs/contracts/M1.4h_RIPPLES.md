# M1.4h — Ripples: the water's surface moves

**Status:** built 2026-09-21, his eyes owed; opened 2026-09-21 on William's "ripples now", which calls in the debt opened on 2026-09-20 ("Still water
reads as flat plates of sky") and takes with it the frame-cost measurement M1.4g's exit record owed. Owner: Claude.

**The one sentence:** M1.4g's water gives the sky back by Fresnel, which is what makes water look like water, but its
surface is perfectly flat, so at any distance a lake or the sea is a mirror in facets rather than a moving surface;
this slice gives the surface a slow movement that breaks the reflection, driven by the wind the weather already knows.

## What is wrong now, and why

`EarthGame/Water` lights every pixel with one normal, straight up. The Fresnel term, the sun's glint and the sky
sample all read that normal, so nothing on the surface ever changes from one frame to the next: a still lake at noon
and the same lake in a gale look the same, and at a distance the surface is a plate. Ripples were a stated non-goal
of M1.4g, kept out so that the column, the Fresnel and the night's darkness could be judged on their own.

## Promises

1. **A ripple field in the shader, nothing moved on the mesh.** The surface normal at a pixel is tilted by the slopes
   of a handful of directional waves (wavelengths from under half a metre to a few metres) summed at the pixel's world
   position and the game's time. Only the normal changes: the Fresnel, the glint and the sky sample take the tilted
   normal, so the reflection breaks and moves. Vertices are not displaced, so the mesh, the wading and the depth column
   are untouched.
2. **The waves move as water waves do.** Each wave's phase speed follows the deep-water dispersion relation
   (ω² = g k): a wavelength of one metre travels at about 1.25 m/s, a three-metre one at about 2.2 m/s. One law, no
   tuned speeds.
3. **The wind drives them.** The client sets the water material's wind each second from `Weather.At` (the wind the
   recorder already reads: `WindMs`, `WindFromDeg`): the waves run downwind, the surface is nearly still in a calm and
   its slopes grow with the wind to a cap. A still morning and a windy afternoon on the same lake look different.
4. **No shimmer at a distance.** Each wave fades out where it would be finer than the pixels that draw it, the short
   ones first, so the far water does not sparkle with aliasing.
5. **The cost stays in the shader** and is measured: a few sines per water pixel. The frame cost at the lake vantage,
   with `-eg-hold 20`, twice on fresh worlds, with the ripples and with `-eg-hide water` — which also pays the
   measurement M1.4g owed.

## Non-goals

Vertex displacement or any wave with height; wakes behind a wading founder; foam, a shore wash or breaking waves; the
sound of water; refraction of the bed; reflections of the land; the sea's swell as a thing apart from wind ripples.

## Exit record (2026-09-21)

**What exists.** `EarthGame/Water` tilts its normal by the slopes of four waves (wavelengths 0.45, 0.9, 1.7 and 3.1 m)
summed at the pixel's world position, each running downwind at the deep-water dispersion's speed and fading over the
distance at which it would be finer than the pixels; the wind's gusts, patches tens of metres across carried downwind
at the wind's speed, make the field rougher and calmer from place to place; and where the ripples are too fine to draw
their root-mean-square slope is added to the Fresnel's facing, so the far water gives back less sky the rougher it is,
in moving patches, instead of standing as one plate. The mesh, the wading and the depth column are untouched. The
client sets the material's wind once a second from `Weather.At` (downwind and the speed) and the ripples' own time,
which runs only while the game is awake, so a paused game's water stands with its world. `-eg-hide water` draws no
water, for the cost; `-eg-day D` and `-eg-hour H` ask a development game for a day of the year and an hour at launch,
sent once as the developer's clock settings when the mode is granted, so frames can be taken in wind on a world whose
first day is still. Every frame's record carries the wind it was taken in (`wind_ms`, `wind_from_deg`).
