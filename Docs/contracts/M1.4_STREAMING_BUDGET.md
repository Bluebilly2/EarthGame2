# Contract M1.4e — The client's streaming work keeps to a budget

**Status:** opened 2026-09-10 after M1.4d closed. Owner: Claude (Opus 5). The owner's lane: the frames, because
this slice changes how finely the ground is drawn, and he says whether anything he cares about was lost. The
budget itself is not his to judge: it is ARCHITECTURE §8's number and it is either kept or it is not.

M1.4a moved world creation off the main thread and left a debt in DEBTS.md: "client view creation is still
synchronous… this finishing work has no frame-time budget yet." Nobody had measured it. This slice measures it,
cuts what is waste, moves what can be moved, and puts a stopwatch across what is left.

## What was measured before it was designed

One tile's worth of work, in Release, on this machine (`dotnet run` over `EarthGame.ClientCore`):

| what happens when a tile arrives | cost |
|---|---|
| sampling a tile's posts for the Terrain, 1025 a side | **65.6 ms** |
| the same at 513 a side | 21.9 ms |
| the ground's colour map, 512 texels a side (M1.4d) | 7.1 ms |
| the same at 256 texels | 2.7 ms |

Against ARCHITECTURE §8: streaming is capped at **1.5 ms of CPU a frame**, and a frame is 13.3 ms by design and
33 ms before it is a named defect. One tile is five frames of work today, and a join builds nine.

The 1025 is waste rather than detail: a streamed tile carries posts every 4 m, and 1025 a side over a kilometre
interpolates that at 0.98 m — four samples for every one the world has. `TerrainTileBuilder`'s own doc comment
has said 513 since M1.B ("a post every 1.95 m, sampled bilinearly from the tile's 4 m posts") while the constant
beside it said 1025: one fact in two places, and the expensive copy won.

### Amended the same day, before anything was proved against it

The paragraph above read the code wrong, and the correction is written here rather than quietly applied.
`ClientRuntime` had a **third** copy of the number, `TilePosts = 513`, and it was the one the streamed tiles were
actually built at; `TerrainTileBuilder.Posts = 1025` was what the coarse ring and the far skirt used. One name
over two facts, which is the same bug shape from the other side.

So the figures mean this: **21.9 ms** is what a streamed tile has been costing all along, on the main thread,
against a 1.5 ms budget — the substance of this slice is unchanged and promises 2 to 7 stand as written.
**65.6 ms** is what the ring and the skirt each cost, once at a join; they are a stated non-goal here and the
rest of the DEBTS row Codex opened. Promise 1 therefore becomes: **the two facts get two names**
(`TerrainTileBuilder.TilePosts = 513` for a streamed tile, `CoarsePosts = 1025` for the ring and the skirt), the
duplicate in `ClientRuntime` is deleted, and **no pitch changes** — so the ground is drawn exactly as it was and
the frames of promise 6 are a check that nothing moved, not a judgement of something new.

## What this slice promises

1. **One name per pitch.** `TerrainTileBuilder.TilePosts` (513, a streamed tile: 1.95 m, two posts for every
   4 m post the wire carried) and `TerrainTileBuilder.CoarsePosts` (1025, the ring and the skirt), with
   `ClientRuntime`'s duplicate deleted. Neither number changes, so nothing is drawn differently; see the
   amendment above for what the first reading of this got wrong.
2. **What can leave the main thread, leaves it.** Sampling the posts and building the colour map are pure
   functions over what the client already holds, in `EarthGame.ClientCore`, with no Unity type in sight. They run
   on a worker; the main thread does only what Unity requires of it — `SetHeights`, the texture, the objects.
   A tile whose worker result arrives while the client is dropping that tile is discarded, not built.
3. **A stopwatch, not an item count.** The main thread does streaming work until the budget for that frame is
   spent and then stops until the next one, as ARCHITECTURE §8 says: a stopwatch budget, never an item count.
   The rule that the tile under the founder is built first survives it.
4. **What it cost is recorded.** `run.jsonl` gains a `build` record per tile — the layer, the tile, the
   milliseconds on the worker, the milliseconds on the main thread, and the frame it landed in — and the
   `interactive` record gains the worst main-thread frame seen up to that point. The format's row in
   ARCHITECTURE §10 is amended in the same commit; this is additive to version 1.
5. **A verifier, `build_check.py`**, reads a run's `build` records and reports the worst and the 95th-percentile
   main-thread cost against §8's budget, and the total worker time against the wall-clock time to interactive —
   the second is what says the work really moved rather than merely being renamed. It reads `run.jsonl` alone and
   computes its own percentile; the client's summary of itself is not evidence.
6. **The named defect class is measured again.** The client's collider is the Terrain built here, and this slice
   changes when it is built, on what thread, and in what order. The walk scenario runs and corrections must stay
   inside N2 (≤ 2 a minute on the divergence segment, no displacement over 1.0 m); the number is stated either
   way.
7. **Tests:** the sampler and the colour map against a made tile off the main thread (the same answer as before,
   since they are pure); the budget's own arithmetic — work stops when the budget is spent and resumes with what
   was left; a result for a tile no longer held is dropped.

## Non-goals

The tile decode itself (inflate and unpack), which happens where the message arrives and is not yet measured.
The coarse ring and the skirt, built once at join. Anything about how the ground looks: the palette, the water
and the atmosphere are not touched. Making Unity's `SetHeights` cheaper, which is Unity's own cost and is
measured here rather than fixed.

**What moved and what did not.** 92 ms of every 309 is now off the main thread, and what is left is 93 %
`TerrainData.SetHeights`. That call is 21 ms for a 513-post tile and there is no cheaper way to say it:
`SetHeightsDelayLOD` with `SyncHeightmap` was measured the same day at 21 to 25 ms, the same work under two
names. So a tile is still a frame of about 30 ms; the budget's achievement is that it is **one** tile, not nine.
The rest is a debt with its number in DEBTS.md, and `build_check`'s third row keeps anything new from growing
beside it unnoticed.

**A defect found on the way, and fixed.** A tile's posts were sampled through the field of held tiles, and a
tile's last post is its neighbour's first: at the edge of what a client holds, the neighbour is not held, the
field answered NaN, and 1,025 posts of every outermost tile were built from nothing. A tile is now sampled from
itself. `ThePreparedGroundIsTheGroundTheFieldWouldHaveGiven` pins the two paths together post for post and counts
exactly those 1,025 as the difference.

**The frames** are at `Artefacts/frames/m14e/{wake,shore,windermere}/frames/`, the same three vantages and the
same commands as M1.4d. They are **not** a pixel comparison with M1.4d's, and this is worth saying plainly: every
host run advances the world's clock, so these were recorded at 09:16 where M1.4d's were at 08:14. A higher sun,
brighter sand, shorter shadows, and 19 levels of mean difference over ground that did not move a centimetre. That
nothing moved is proved by the test above, on the heights themselves; the frames show only that the country still
reads as the same country. The clock is now a debt of its own.

## What changed on the way

- **The contract's own premise was half wrong** and is amended above rather than quietly fixed: `ClientRuntime`
  held a third copy of the post count and the streamed tiles were already built at 513. No pitch changed; the two
  facts got two names.
- **The first frames came back one flat tan.** On a join over the in-memory transport the small cover tile lands
  before its ground is built, and neither path then asked for it: the ground had no cover when it was prepared,
  and the cover's own path required a terrain that did not yet exist. A tile now asks for its cover when it is
  built without one.
- **`TakeColour` would have dropped a pending tile (0, 0).** It removed by an id that was still the default when
  nothing had finished. Found by reading it, not by a run.
- **The budget was reported at the wrong instant, twice.** First `frame_ms` was read after the log line, so the
  log's own cost read as budget overrun; then the spend was read at the top of the work, which includes compiling
  that work on the way in — 3.26 ms of JIT read as the budget allowing work on a spent frame. Asking the budget
  is now the decision, and it hands back what it decided on.
