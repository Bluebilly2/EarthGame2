# Contract M1.4e — The client's streaming work keeps to a budget

**Status:** opened 2026-09-10 after M1.4d closed. Owner: Claude (Opus 5). The owner's lane: the frames, because
this slice changes how finely the ground is drawn, and he says whether anything he cares about was lost. The
budget itself is not his to judge: it is ARCHITECTURE §8's number and it is either kept or it is not.

M1.4a moved world creation off the main thread and left a debt in DEBTS.md: "client view creation is still
synchronous… this finishing work has no frame-time budget yet." Nobody had measured it. This slice measures it,
cuts what is waste, moves what can be moved, and puts a stopwatch across what is left.

## What was measured before it was designed

One tile's worth of work, in Release, on this machine (`dotnet run` over `EarthGame.ClientCore`):

| what happens when a tile arrives | cost |
|---|---|
| sampling a tile's posts for the Terrain, 1025 a side | **65.6 ms** |
| the same at 513 a side | 21.9 ms |
| the ground's colour map, 512 texels a side (M1.4d) | 7.1 ms |
| the same at 256 texels | 2.7 ms |

Against ARCHITECTURE §8: streaming is capped at **1.5 ms of CPU a frame**, and a frame is 13.3 ms by design and
33 ms before it is a named defect. One tile is five frames of work today, and a join builds nine.

The 1025 is waste rather than detail: a streamed tile carries posts every 4 m, and 1025 a side over a kilometre
interpolates that at 0.98 m — four samples for every one the world has. `TerrainTileBuilder`'s own doc comment
has said 513 since M1.B ("a post every 1.95 m, sampled bilinearly from the tile's 4 m posts") while the constant
beside it said 1025: one fact in two places, and the expensive copy won.

## What this slice promises

1. **The ground is built at two posts per post the world sent.** `TerrainTileBuilder.Posts` becomes 513, which
   is 1.95 m over a kilometre tile, the number its doc comment has always claimed and the same rule the colour
   map already follows. What this costs is a silhouette resolved at 2 m rather than 1 m over a raster whose own
   pitch is 4 m; the frames show what that looks like.
2. **What can leave the main thread, leaves it.** Sampling the posts and building the colour map are pure
   functions over what the client already holds, in `EarthGame.ClientCore`, with no Unity type in sight. They run
   on a worker; the main thread does only what Unity requires of it — `SetHeights`, the texture, the objects.
   A tile whose worker result arrives while the client is dropping that tile is discarded, not built.
3. **A stopwatch, not an item count.** The main thread does streaming work until the budget for that frame is
   spent and then stops until the next one, as ARCHITECTURE §8 says: a stopwatch budget, never an item count.
   The rule that the tile under the founder is built first survives it.
4. **What it cost is recorded.** `run.jsonl` gains a `build` record per tile — the layer, the tile, the
   milliseconds on the worker, the milliseconds on the main thread, and the frame it landed in — and the
   `interactive` record gains the worst main-thread frame seen up to that point. The format's row in
   ARCHITECTURE §10 is amended in the same commit; this is additive to version 1.
5. **A verifier, `build_check.py`**, reads a run's `build` records and reports the worst and the 95th-percentile
   main-thread cost against §8's budget, and the total worker time against the wall-clock time to interactive —
   the second is what says the work really moved rather than merely being renamed. It reads `run.jsonl` alone and
   computes its own percentile; the client's summary of itself is not evidence.
6. **The named defect class is measured again.** The client's collider is the Terrain built here, and halving its
   resolution moves where the client thinks the ground is. The walk scenario runs and corrections must stay
   inside N2 (≤ 2 a minute on the divergence segment, no displacement over 1.0 m); the number is stated either
   way.
7. **Tests:** the sampler and the colour map against a made tile off the main thread (the same answer as before,
   since they are pure); the budget's own arithmetic — work stops when the budget is spent and resumes with what
   was left; a result for a tile no longer held is dropped.

## Non-goals

The tile decode itself (inflate and unpack), which happens where the message arrives and is not yet measured.
The coarse ring and the skirt, built once at join. Anything about how the ground looks: the palette, the water
and the atmosphere are not touched. Making Unity's `SetHeights` cheaper, which is Unity's own cost and is
measured here rather than fixed.

## How it is proved

- `dotnet test Engine/EarthGame.slnx`: exit 0, the count as run, the new suite by name.
- The Unity-layer compile green.
- The shaped join over the M1.B socket (`Tools/world/stream.py`): time to interactive, and the worst main-thread
  frame, before and after, both stated.
- `python Tools/verifiers/checks/build_check.py`: exit 0, both numbers printed.
- `python Tools/gate/run_gate.py M1.4-streaming` green, with `build_check` among its steps.
- The walk scenario's corrections against N2.
- Frames at both resolutions from the three vantages of M1.4d, so the owner can put them beside yesterday's.
