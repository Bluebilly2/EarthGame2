# M1.4g — Water you can see

**Status:** opened 2026-09-20, on William's rulings 35 ("can you make the water surface partially transparent so
that it doesnt just look like the ground but just blue"), his report the same evening that "at night time, the
water stays bright when it is dark", and his "that order looks good" to taking this first. It also pays the debt
opened that morning, "A creek is drunk from and never drawn".

**The one sentence:** water in this world is judged, waded and drunk from correctly and *shown* wrongly — a creek
is not drawn at all, a lake is an opaque blue lid, and at night the sea is the brightest thing in the frame. This
slice makes what the eye sees agree with what the world already knows.

## What is wrong now, and why

1. **A channel narrower than two cells is never drawn.** `WaterSurface.Build` makes a cell's rectangle only when
   all four of its corner posts are wet by at least `MinDepthM` (1 cm), and takes the lowest of the four as the
   surface, so that a flat sheet never climbs a rising beach. The world's posts stand 4 m apart and WG.1's creeks
   are one cell wide, so no cell along them has four wet corners and nothing is built: in the daylight drink run
   of 2026-09-20 the client logged water on one of the nine tiles it held — the sea's — while the founder stood
   in the creek, drank from it and felt water underfoot thirteen times.
2. **The surface is opaque.** `Sea.mat` is URP Lit, surface type opaque, base colour (0.05, 0.16, 0.24),
   smoothness 0.92. Nothing of the bed, the shallows or the depth shows: a lake reads as blue ground.
3. **At night the water keeps a daytime sky.** `SunAndSky.Apply` moves the sun, the three ambient colours, the fog
   and the skybox's exposure with the solar elevation, so rough surfaces darken. The *reflection* a smooth surface
   shows comes from the environment cubemap, which Unity generates from the skybox once and nothing refreshes
   (`DynamicGI.UpdateEnvironment` is called nowhere in the tree). At smoothness 0.92 the water is nearly a mirror,
   so at 19:26 it mirrors a midday sky: William's screenshot of that evening shows the sea as a pale band brighter
   than the sky above it, in a frame where everything else is nearly black.

## What this slice promises

1. **A creek one cell wide is drawn, and a beach still is not.** A cell is drawn when **any** of its four corner
   posts is wet, and **every corner stands at its own height**: a wet corner at its water's surface, a dry corner on
   the ground itself, a millimetre under it so the two do not fight for a pixel. A creek's surface therefore falls
   with its bed, and because neighbouring cells share their posts the skin is continuous along a channel; on a shore
   it tapers to nothing exactly where the bank begins, so water never laps over dry ground. A body whose cell is
   wholly under water and level keeps the merged flat run it has had since M1.4c. `MinDepthM` stays 1 cm: below that
   there is nothing to draw.

   *What changed on the way (2026-09-20).* This promise was first built as one flat height a cell — the highest wet
   corner's, clamped to the lowest dry corner's ground. It drew the creek and William's own frame showed why that is
   not enough: a creek runs downhill, so the flat plates stepped down the slope as a staircase of panes with gaps
   between them, some standing proud of the ground at their lower edge. Flat quads cannot carry a sloping stream.
   The cornered surface above replaces them, and needs no clamp.
2. **The surface is see-through, and its clarity is the water's own column.** `EarthGame/Water` fades by
   Beer–Lambert over the water between the surface and whatever opaque thing the scene's depth buffer says lies
   behind it, reaching nine tenths of its colour at **1.5 m** of that column. The bed shows through the shallows and
   the deep reads as water, not as a lid.

   *What changed on the way (2026-09-20).* Two things. The column is read from the scene's depth rather than carried
   on the mesh as this promise first said: one number then serves a lake's plane, a four-metre creek and the sea
   alike, it lengthens as the eye looks along the water rather than into it, as a real sightline does, and it gives
   the waterline for nothing. And fading alone was not enough to make water look like water: ankle-deep water is
   honestly sixteen parts in a hundred opaque, so the first build drew the creek and only 1.4 % of the frame moved.
   The surface also gives back the sky by Schlick's approximation on water's normal-incidence reflectance of 0.02 —
   a fiftieth of it to an eye looking straight down, nearly all of it to an eye looking along the water, which is
   what tells anyone that a puddle is a puddle. With it, 5.2 % of the frame moved and the creek read as a creek. The
   1.5 m is the water this country drains rather than open coast: a creek out of peaty heath carries its stain.
3. **At night, water is as dark as the country it lies in.** Proved by frames of the same water by day and after
   dark, with the water's own mean brightness read from the image and stated beside the ground's.

   *What changed on the way (2026-09-20).* The fix is not a refreshed environment, as this promise first proposed,
   but no environment at all: the water takes its light from the same spherical harmonics and main light as every
   other surface in the game, and samples no reflection probe. The stale cubemap then cannot reach it, nothing has
   to be rebuilt as the sun moves, and the sky the water gives back is the one `SunAndSky` is already the owner of.
4. **What it costs a frame stays inside the budget.** Transparent water is measured against the same rule the
   trees are (ARCHITECTURE §8): the water's share of a frame at the lake vantage, day and night, at 1440p and
   1080p, stated as measured, under **1.0 ms** of GPU time.
5. **Nothing else about water changes.** What the server judges, what the founder wades and swims in, what the
   depth tiles carry and what the drink verb answers are untouched: this slice is drawing alone, and the drink
   scenario's own check must stay green on the same world.

## Non-goals

Ripples, waves or any movement on the surface; refraction; foam or a shoreline wash; caustics; reflections of the
land or of anything but the sky; underwater fog or a view from beneath the surface; the ground's own grain (its
own debt); rivers with current.

## How it is proved

- **Headless tests, written first**, on `WaterSurface.Build`: a channel one post wide falls with its bed and its
  cells share their corners, so the skin is continuous; a shore tapers to the sand and the cell beyond it is not
  drawn; every corner keeps its own post's surface; two levels never merge into one; a millimetre of noise draws
  nothing; the sea's own datum is left to the sea's plane. Each rule broken once and seen red before it is trusted
  (STANDARDS 7).
- **The drink scenario** re-run on a copy of the gate world: `thirst_check` green on every row, and the client's
  own log showing water built on the tile the creek crosses.
- **Frames** at the creek by the wake, at Lake Windermere's margin and at the shore, at 1440p and 1080p, in
  daylight and an hour after sunset, with the renderer's state logged beside them; the water's mean brightness
  read from the night frames and stated against the ground's.
- **The frame cost** measured with `-eg-hold 20` at the lake vantage, twice on fresh worlds, the faster median
  kept, with and without the water hidden.
- **William's eyes** on the day and night frames, and on the creek when he next plays: ruling 35 is his to close.

## Exit record (2026-09-20)

- **The rule, tested and sabotaged.** `WaterSurface.Build` gives every corner its own height. Six tests over the
  cases the promise names, written before the code and seen red; then four rules broken once each by sabotage and
  seen red again, the source restored byte for byte each time (`water_sabotage2.py`). The first sabotage of the
  merging rule was a no-op — a cell that is not flat cannot open a run anyway — and was replaced by one that widens
  `SameSurfaceM` until two levels merge. 715 tests green over the whole tree, the Unity-shaped compile clean.
- **Drawn where it was not.** In the daylight drink run the client built water on **six** of the nine tiles it held,
  78 to 386 rectangles each, where the same run on the old rule built five rectangles on one tile — the sea's.
- **Seen.** `Artefacts/frames/drink-20260920T082852Z` (the creek at the wake, 10:01),
  `Artefacts/frames/vantages-20260920T081108Z/windermere` (the lake, 10:30),
  `Artefacts/frames/drink-20260920T080216Z` (the sea, 20:28). Against the first build of the slice, 5.2 % of the
  creek frame's pixels moved by more than 8 levels in 255; against the build before the slice, the creek is a
  continuous ribbon where there was ground.
- **Dark after dark, measured.** In William's own screenshot of the old build at 19:26 the water read 54.9 of 255
  against the ground's 20.9 beside it — 2.6 times, its brightest pixel 238. On this build at 20:28 the same
  comparison is 19.0 against 15.5 — 1.2 times, brightest pixel 21. (The two views differ; the ratio is the claim.)
- **Nothing else moved.** `thirst_check` green on every row on a fresh copy of the gate world, the founder drinking
  from the creek and refused by the sea exactly as before.
- **Judged by William, in part.** He saw the first build's staircase and said so, which is what the cornered surface
  answers; of the second: "the streams are not perfect and there are things wrong with them, but we will fix that
  later". Ruling 35 is not yet closed.

### Owed

- **What it costs a frame** (promise 4), unmeasured: a timing is worthless beside a running game, and his own game
  was open from the moment the slice was buildable. To be measured with `-eg-hold 20` at the lake vantage when the
  machine is quiet — which is itself easier once M1.E's idle pause makes an open game cost nothing.
- **The creek is drawn wider than it is**, about a cell either side of its channel where the real one is nearer two
  metres, because the channel is one post of a 4 m raster. Its own debt row.
- **The distant water reads as flat plates of sky**, having no ripple to break the reflection. Ripples were a stated
  non-goal here. Its own debt row.
- **William's eyes** on the creek walked along its length, the shallows where it meets the sand, and the sea at
  dusk.
