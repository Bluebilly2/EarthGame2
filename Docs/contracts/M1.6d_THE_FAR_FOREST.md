# Contract M1.6d — The far forest

**Status:** opened 2026-09-13 on the owner's "continue", after M1.6c, as the M1.6 draft's last slice
(`M1.6_THE_REST_OF_THE_FLORA.md`). Owner: Claude (Opus 5). The owner's lane: whether the country reads as forested to
the horizon, and the frames.

## What was found before it was designed (2026-09-13)

- **The forest ends in a line.** A client asks for the nine kilometre tiles round the founder, of every layer, and
  lets go of the farthest when it holds more than twenty-five of a layer (`GameClient.RequestTilesAround`,
  `TileReceiver.Trim`). Trees are drawn from the stand tiles it holds, so none stands beyond about 1.5 km, and past
  that the coarse ring of the region is bare ground drawn from the bake (DEBTS, "no tree stands beyond the nine
  tiles").
- **The server would serve any tile.** `TileService.Serve` answers any tile of the region's grid for any layer the
  world has, with no interest test, and caches what it encodes; so a coarser layer for the whole region costs the
  server one aggregation a tile, once.
- **The stand is too fine to send whole.** It is a byte a 4 m cell over 8 km, two thousand cells a side; the nine
  tiles round the wake alone are 110,056 bytes as cached. A square of ten cells by ten, 40 m a side, is coarse enough
  to send the whole region cheaply and fine enough to shape a canopy seen from a kilometre off.
- **The far band already draws thinned trees.** Beyond a kilometre `StandViews` draws one tree in four with its crown
  doubled, from a three-sided trunk under one clump; a canopy seen from further off needs no more than that.
- **The contracted formats this touches.** The tile layers and the wire protocol (ARCHITECTURE §10); `tile_check`
  reads every cached tile and refuses a folder it does not know.

## What this slice promises

1. **Two coarse layers on the wire.** Tile layer 6, the far stand, carries for each post 40 m apart the tall plant
   most of the trees in the square round it are, at their mean height, in `StandCodes`' own packing; layer 7, the far
   count, carries how many trees stand in that square. The server works both out from the world's stand layer when a
   tile is first asked for; nothing is added to the world folder. Protocol version 8.
2. **The whole region, once.** A client asks for the far layers of every tile of the region at the join, after the
   nine tiles it stands in, and never lets them go.
3. **Trees to the edge of the region.** Where the client holds no stand tile, it draws the far forest instead: one far
   tree for each square that holds any, standing inside its square where the square's hash puts it, on the ground
   the client already draws there, as tall as the square's trees are on average and with a crown as wide as all
   their crowns together would cover. When the founder walks and a tile's real stand arrives, the far forest of that
   tile gives way to it, and comes back when the stand is let go.
4. **Measured.** The frame's cost with and without the far forest (`-eg-hide ring`), as M1.6a's and M1.6c's were: the
   recorder's `-eg-hold` on freshly created worlds, the faster of two passes, the passes in opposite orders.
5. **Seen.** Frames at the four vantages, 1440p and 1080p, windowless and muted.
6. **Checked.** `tile_check` learns the two folders and holds every far post against the world's own stand layer, by
   an aggregation of its own in Python.
7. **Tests.** A far square's count and its plant and height are the stand's, and neighbouring squares never count a
   tree twice; the far trees of a tile stand inside their squares, as wide as their trees' crowns would cover and
   never wider than a square allows; a client asks for the whole region's far layers once and keeps them through a
   trim; the far layers arrive post for post from a server.

Pays DEBTS' "no tree stands beyond the nine tiles".

## Non-goals

The ground's colour beyond the tiles (DEBTS, "the country's colour stops where the streamed tiles do", which needs
the cover to travel the same way and is left for its own slice); trees in the 64 km skirt beyond the region, which no
world holds; the understorey or the litter far off; shadows from far trees.

## How it is proved

`dotnet test` and the Unity-layer compile, the count as run; `python Tools/unity/editmode.py`; the cost by `-eg-hold`
with and without the far forest on freshly created worlds; `tile_check` on a client's cache; the frames at the four
vantages; the owner's eyes.

## What it did (exit record, 2026-09-13)

1. **Two coarse layers on the wire.** Tile layer 6, the far stand, and layer 7, the far count, have their posts 40 m
   apart (`TileLayers.FarCellM`), twenty-six a kilometre tile's side. The server works both out from the world's stand
   when a tile is first asked for (`TileCodec.FarSquare`), and the host before it listens: a far post carries the
   square of stand cells from half a square before its cell to just short of half a square after, ten 4 m cells a
   side on this world, so that no tree is counted by two posts; the far count is how many trees stand in the square,
   to a byte, and the far stand the tall plant most of them are, the first in `StandCodes.Tall`'s order on a tie,
   packed by `StandCodes.Pack` at their mean height. Nothing is added to the world folder, and a stand whose cells do
   not make a 40 m square whole is served no far layer (`TileCodec.FarSpan`). Protocol version 8; a tile request
   names at most 64 tiles (`TileRequestMessage.MostTiles`), and the whole region goes in as many as it needs.
2. **The whole region, once.** At the join the client asks for the far layers of every tile of the region's grid,
   after the tiles round it (`GameClient.RequestTilesAround`), and `TileReceiver.Trim` passes them over, so they are
   kept wherever the founder walks. Like the water, they gate nothing: interactive is still the ground under the
   founder.
3. **Trees to the edge of the region.** For each far square that holds a tree, `FarForest.Place` stands one far tree
   inside the square, within a quarter of a square of its post where a hash of the post's place in whole centimetres
   puts it, as tall as the square's trees are on average and with a crown as wide as all of theirs together would
   cover — one tree's crown by the square root of how many — never wider than a square and a quarter, on the region's
   bake, the ground the coarse ring is drawn from. `StandViews` places each tile's far forest on a worker, sorts it
   into quarter-tile blocks, and draws the blocks in the view in the far band's own mesh and material, unthinned and
   casting no shadow, only while that tile's own stand is not held: when a stand tile arrives, its far forest gives
   way to the real trees, and it comes back when the tile is let go. `-eg-hide ring` draws none of it, and
   `-eg-hide far` hides it with the far band.
4. **Measured.** The recorder's `-eg-hold 20` at the wake, beside Windermere and at the new wake, on two freshly created
   worlds, each configuration the faster of two passes and the passes in opposite orders
   (`Artefacts/frames/m16d-hold-{with,without}-{a,b}`): the median frame is 7.4 ms with the far forest and 7.2 ms
   without it at the wake, 8.2 against 8.1 beside Windermere, and 7.5 against 7.2 at the new wake. **The far forest
   costs 0.1 to 0.3 ms of the median frame.** Its 95th-percentile frame is up to 1.4 ms slower (11.3 against 9.9 ms at
   the wake, 11.0 against 9.8 at the new wake, 10.7 against 10.7 beside Windermere). Without the far forest the frame
   measured higher than in M1.6c's exit record (7.2 ms at the new wake and 8.1 beside Windermere, against 6.7 and 6.8),
   so M1.6c's own player (`Build/Player-M16c2`) was run again beside this build with the far forest hidden, the same way
   on two more fresh worlds: 6.9 ms against 6.9 at the new wake and 7.9 against 7.9 beside Windermere
   (`Artefacts/frames/m16d-control-*`). The rise is the day's, not the slice's: with the far forest hidden this build's
   frame is M1.6c's.
5. **Seen.** From the ground the far forest hardly shows. At the wake, beside Windermere and at the new wake, the first
   frame with it and the first without it, taken at the same hour of fresh worlds, differ in 0.00 to 0.13 per cent of
   their pixels, because from inside the forest the trees round the founder hide everything a kilometre off (the walk
   and turn frames are no comparison: a walked founder stands a little elsewhere in each run). So the recorder learned a
   lookout (`-eg-lookout`, run by `vantages.py --lookout 150`): a development game's founder flies up 150 m over where
   the turn left them and looks out to each point of the compass. From there the frames with the far forest and without
   it differ in 0.8 to 3.1 per cent of their pixels, every one of them from the horizon down: without it the forest
   stops at the held tiles, about a kilometre and a half out, and bare ground runs on to the region's edge; with it the
   trees go on to the edge, and no line shows where the held tiles end (`Artefacts/frames/m16d-lookout`, each view
   without and with side by side and its changed pixels painted red; the frames themselves, 1440p and 1080p, in
   `m16d-lookout-with-d` and `m16d-lookout-without-e`). The frames from the ground at the four vantages are
   `Artefacts/frames/m16d-vantages`.
6. **Checked.** `tile_check` learns the two folders. It holds every far post against squares it counts itself, over
   the whole raster at once in numpy, every cell into the one square it falls in, where the game works out each
   post's square on its own; it holds that the far layers cover the whole region, their cached counts adding up to
   the trees the stand holds; and it keeps the far layers out of the residency bound.
7. **Tests.** A far square's count, plant and mean height are the stand's, a tree just past a square is the next
   square's, every tree is counted by one far post, and a far tile carries its posts' squares (`FarLayerTests`); one
   far tree stands inside each square that holds any, as wide as its trees' crowns would cover, and each square is
   placed by one tile and an empty square by none (`FarForestTests`); a client joining a region of more tiles than it
   keeps of a layer is sent the far layers of all of them, keeps them, and holds what the stand's squares say post for
   post, and a stand of 15 m cells serves no far layer (`FarLayerStreamingTests`). The protocol's version test and the
   refusals counted for a world without ground moved with them.

**Evidence** (2026-09-13; every run muted and windowless, on freshly created worlds. The second shaped join and the
timing passes ran the player `Tools/world/stream.py --build` made and copied to `Build/Player-M16d2`; the lookout
ran `Build/Player-M16d3`, which adds it; the frames from the ground are the first build's, `Build/Player-M16d`). `dotnet test Engine/EarthGame.slnx`: 395 passed. The
Unity-layer compile: 0 warnings, 0 errors. Edit-mode: 8 of 8.

*On the wire.* The shaped join (CANON ruling 11's first condition: 100 ms round trip, 20 ms of jitter, 2 % loss, a
10 Mbit/s cap) became interactive 1.40 s after connecting, where the shaped joins of 2026-09-10 and 2026-09-11 took
1.29 to 1.43 s (and once 1.88), and received 735,463 bytes in its minute. The whole region's far stand is 14,257
bytes as cached and its far count 25,297, beside 282,686 for the nine tiles of ground. `tile_check` on that client's
cache is green in every row: 0 posts disagree of the 64 far-stand tiles and the 64 far-count tiles, and the cached
far counts add up to 992,519 trees where the stand holds 992,519 (`Artefacts/streaming/20260913T011018924530Z`; the
build before `FarSpan` gave the same rows, `Artefacts/streaming/20260913T005910392650Z`). A far tile costs the server
about 1 ms to work out, against 7.8 ms for a tile of the stand itself, timed over the region's 64 tiles; the host
encoded its 512 tiles in 3.41 s before it listened. The far forest is 28,842 far trees over the region, as every
vantage run's end record counts them.

**What changed on the way.** The first vantage record called its far trees "beyond the stand tiles"; they are the
whole region's, placed for every tile and drawn where its stand is not held, and the record now says so. A stand
whose cells did not make a 40 m square whole would have thrown inside the server's update the first time a far tile
was asked for; it is now served no far layer, and a test holds that. The frames from the ground could not show what the slice made, so the recorder
learned the lookout; and the lookout showed some far trees standing in open water near the region's north edge,
where the world holds them on sand a few tenths of a metre above the datum and the frame without the far forest
draws the same sand as water (DEBTS, "low ground beside the sea is drawn under the water from afar"). The frame
measured higher without the far forest than in M1.6c's record, which the control run beside M1.6c's own player
parted from the slice.
